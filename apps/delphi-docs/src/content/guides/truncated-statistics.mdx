A sample is **truncated** when every observation outside some set $S$ is
missing entirely: not recorded as missing, just absent. What remains is a
biased view of the distribution that produced it, and statistics computed on
it, from a mean to a least-squares fit, are biased toward $S$.

delphi.ai's truncated models recover the distribution or model as it was
before truncation. They work best with a **membership oracle** for the set: a
plain callable `phi` that answers "is this point observed?" for a batch of
points. When the set cannot be queried at all, every distribution can learn
it from the sample instead: pass `phi=None`.

## What is covered

- **Distributions**: the [normal](/truncated-multivariate-normal) and 30
  other exponential families, continuous, discrete, simplex and
  matrix-valued, each with a known or a learned truncation set, fit by
  projected SGD on the truncated negative log-likelihood.
- **Regression**: [linear](/truncated-linear-regression), with known or
  unknown noise variance or dependent data, and [sparse](/truncated-lasso-regression).
- **Classification**: [probit](/truncated-probit-regression),
  [logistic](/truncated-logistic-regression) and
  [multinomial logistic](/truncated-multinomial-logistic-regression)
  regression, truncated on the latent.
- **Dynamical systems**: a [linear quadratic regulator](/truncated-lqr).

## Learning the truncation set

Every distribution also accepts `phi=None`. It then learns the set first,
following Lee, Mehrotra and Zampetakis (2024): the sample, labeled 1, is mixed
with draws from the family's untruncated fit to it, labeled 0 at weight
`truncation_set_rho`, and a polynomial threshold of degree
`truncation_set_degree` is fit to the labels by L1 regression. The family then
fits the samples inside the learned set by the same procedure as with a known
`phi`. Heavy-tailed families learn the set in $\log x$.

The basis has $\binom{k + d}{d}$ terms, so unless set the degree is the
largest up to 4 whose basis has at most 250 terms: 4 up to 6 dimensions, 3 up
to 9, 2 up to 20, and 1 beyond. A family whose samples satisfy a polynomial
identity, such as the Dirichlet's coordinates summing to one or a Bernoulli's
$x^2 = x$, raises a `ValueError` naming the degree.

```python
import torch as ch
import torch.distributions

from delphi.truncated.distributions import truncated_exponential

ch.manual_seed(0)

# Observed only above 1; the fit is never told so.
population = torch.distributions.Exponential(ch.tensor([1.0])).sample([20000])
S = population[population[:, 0] > 1.0][:5000]

model = truncated_exponential.TruncatedExponential(None)
model.fit(S)

rate = model.avg_rate_
# The learned set, as a membership oracle.
inside = model.truncation_set_(ch.tensor([[0.5], [2.0]]))
```

The [normal](/truncated-multivariate-normal) has a second route for an
unknown set, the Hermite expansion of Kontonis, Tzamos and Zampetakis (2019),
which is its default; `method="l1"` selects the route above instead.
