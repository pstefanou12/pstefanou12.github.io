Summary

UFC Fight Night: Medić vs. Rodriguez was a great event, even if the level of competition varied wildly from top to bottom.


No fanbase shows up quite like the European crowds, and Belgrade lived up to that reputation completely, flares in the stands and an electric building from the first fight to the last. The fights themselves were entertaining across the board. The one real gripe with the card is a matchmaking issue more than anything else: the UFC clearly had to sign way too many new fighters just to get enough Serbian presence on the card, and it showed. Some of that talent was low level, and there were a handful of genuine mismatches sprinkled throughout the night.


The main event delivered exactly what it needed to. Uroš Medić needed just thirty seconds to blast through Daniel Rodriguez, reinforcing that Medic might never be a champion but is always must-see television, a live-by-the-sword, die-by-the-sword fighter who's ready to put on a show every time out. Rodriguez, on the other hand, never should have been hyped the way he was, and an eight-month stint in prison clearly didn't help matters heading into this one.


The co-main event turned into the story of the night for a different reason. Navajo Stirling wasn't expected to win this one, but he ground down Jan Błachowicz's legs before finishing him with ground and pound in round one. This felt like the fight where Błachowicz's last several years of injuries finally caught up with him on camera; he's on a serious skid, and it might be time to call it a career.


The rest of the main card was a mixed bag. Aleksandar Rakić outpointed a totally shot Marcin Tybura, but it wasn't the kind of performance that gets him back into the heavyweight title picture after four straight losses, and the third round in particular was a snoozer. The rest of the card was littered with first-round finishes, so many that it started to feel less like a coincidence and more like a trend by the end of the night.


Final Score: 7.1/10

Main Card

Uroš Medić vs. Daniel Rodriguez

Result: TKO (punches) at R1 0:30

Daniel Rodriguez opened well, catching Uroš Medić with a low kick that briefly buckled him before either man really got going. It didn't matter. Medic answered with a vicious combination, capped by a short left hand on the chin, and Rodriguez hit the deck. Medic followed him down and unloaded until the referee had seen enough, needing just thirty seconds to end the night.


It was the kind of finish that reinforces exactly what Medic is at this point in his career: a live-by-the-sword, die-by-the-sword fighter who is never boring. He's probably not a UFC champion in the making, but he's appointment viewing every time he steps in the cage. On the other side, this was the fight to finally put the Rodriguez hype to rest, an eight-month prison stint clearly didn't do him any favors heading into this one. Medic deserves a ranked opponent next, and he's already made the case himself, calling out Leon Edwards after the win. That's a genuinely good matchup.

Navajo Stirling vs. Jan Błachowicz

Result: TKO (elbows and punches) at R1 2:56

Navajo Stirling threw a head kick early that slipped off, and Jan Błachowicz tried to capitalize but couldn't keep him down. From there Stirling settled into a leg-kick heavy gameplan, chopping away round after round. Błachowicz landed a left that briefly stung Stirling, but Stirling clinched immediately and kept working. The leg kicks eventually opened the door: Stirling hurt Błachowicz badly, dropped him, and followed him to the canvas, unloading ground and pound that busted him open before the referee stepped in.


That's a genuinely nasty win for Stirling, one that felt bigger than expected going in. For Błachowicz, this was the fight where the last several years of injuries finally caught up to him on camera. He's been on a serious skid, and this was the first time he really looked like he'd lost a step. It might be time to call it a career. As for Stirling, give him one more fight before anyone starts talking about a title shot.

Aleksandar Rakić vs. Marcin Tybura

Result: Decision (unanimous) (30–27, 30–27, 30–27) at R3 5:00

Aleksandar Rakić looked like the far faster fighter early, tagging Marcin Tybura repeatedly in round one while Tybura simply couldn't close the distance to land anything back. Rakić was busy in that opening round, including a head kick, but he was already visibly slowing by the end of it. Round two turned into a lull: a spinning attack from Rakić didn't do much, Tybura landed some knees in the clinch that stumbled him slightly, but Rakić's output kept dropping. Round three was even quieter, with the two mostly trading in the clinch against the cage before the final bell.


After the opening round, honestly, not much happened, and the third round in particular was a snoozer. This wasn't the performance Rakić needed. He's coming off four straight losses, and a plodding decision over Tybura doesn't do anything to get him back into the heavyweight title conversation. As for Tybura, he's just not good anymore.

Robert Valentin vs. Duško Todorović

Result: Submission (guillotine choke) at R1 4:14

Robert Valentin opened with an immediate body kick that stumbled Duško Todorović, and the fight only got messier from there. Todorović shot for an early takedown and walked right into a guillotine attempt, but scrambled out of it and got back to his feet. He responded by working Valentin against the fence and getting a takedown of his own, only for Valentin to threaten a Von Flue choke from the bottom. The two spent the next stretch jockeying for position before Todorović stood back up and shot for another takedown, this time walking straight into a guillotine that finished the fight for good.


Another first-round finish on a card full of them, which says plenty about the overall level on display. It was just sloppy, low-grade stuff, and neither man did much to inspire confidence going forward.

Gilbert Urbina vs. Vlasto Čepo

Result: TKO (elbows and punches) at R1 1:01

Vlasto Čepo came out charging, pushing Gilbert Urbina back to the fence and letting his hands go early. Urbina weathered the storm and dropped Čepo before following up with a barrage of ground and pound that the referee could not let continue, closing the show in the opening minute of round one.

Noah Gugnon vs. Miloš Janičić

Result: Submission (rear-naked choke) at R1 1:21

It barely lasted a minute. A perfectly placed low kick from Noah Gugnon left Miloš Janičić's lead leg completely dead, and he crumpled to the canvas before Gugnon even had to throw another strike. Gugnon followed him down and locked up a rear-naked choke to end it in seventy-nine seconds.


There must be something in the water in Belgrade, because this card was littered with first-round finishes, an absolutely wild trend by the end of the night. As for Janičić, that was a rough way to go out. Damn, bro. That was pathetic.

Prelims

Tofiq Musayev vs. Ľudovít Klein

Result: TKO (punches) at R2 4:07

Tofiq Musayev set the tone immediately, catching an early body kick from Ľudovít Klein and using it to control the clinch against the cage. From there he went to work on Klein's lead leg with punishing inside kicks, stinging him with a right hook and stumbling him repeatedly, all while controlling the center of the Octagon. Klein started to find a little range in round two, landing a big left hand and a body shot of his own, but it was too little. Musayev answered with a big right that put Klein down, followed him to the canvas, and closed the show with a string of ground and pound that included some shots that drifted to the back of the head.


Musayev is apparently just a dirty fighter, that's the takeaway there, but the bigger story is how one-sided this was. Klein got schooled from the opening bell all the way to the stoppage. It's time to see Musayev step in with a real fighter, someone like Jalin Turner or another ranked name.

Michael Oliveira vs. Oban Elliott

Result: TKO (punches) at R1 1:49

Oban Elliott actually landed a couple of clean right hands early, but that was about the extent of his success. Michael Oliveira pressed forward relentlessly from there, putting Elliott down with a jab of all things. Elliott tried to scramble back to his feet, resorted to desperation takedown attempts, and got clipped again with a right hook that put him away for good less than two minutes into the fight.


Elliott had no business being in the cage with Oliveira, and that performance was beyond embarrassing, it might be time for him to head out of the UFC. Oliveira, meanwhile, looks like he could be a real problem in the welterweight division. Stopping a guy dead in his tracks with a jab is wild stuff.

Borislav Nikolić vs. Mark Vologdin

Result: Decision (unanimous) (29–27, 29–27, 29–27) at R3 5:00

Borislav Nikolić came out extremely aggressive to start, but it was Mark Vologdin who landed first, dropping Nikolić early with a left hook and following up with a headlock full of vicious knees to the head. Nikolić survived, got back to his feet, and the fight turned into a back-and-forth scramble from there, with both men briefly hurt at different points and neither able to fully capitalize. Nikolić started swinging wilder as the fight wore on, tagging Vologdin up against the cage in round two, and by round three he was clearly the sharper fighter, dropping Vologdin with a right hand and following him to the ground with nasty ground and pound. Vologdin somehow kept surviving, getting stuck in a guillotine and later a rear-naked choke attempt but finding a way out of each, standing on unsteady legs against the cage more than once. Nikolić couldn't find the finish, but he didn't need one, sweeping the scorecards after out-working Vologdin over the final two rounds.


Why was Vologdin even signed to the UFC in the first place? Because he has a good chin? He literally lost on the Contender Series. Nikolić stumbled out of the gate but spent rounds two and three just wrecking Vologdin. Vologdin is also carrying too much muscle for his own good and looks like he's in the wrong weight class entirely; if he can't make flyweight, he can't compete in this sport, and he isn't long enough for the division regardless. Nikolić deserves another fight, and he's already called for it himself, calling out Brad Katona, a fighter he's already lost to. Run it back.

Bogdan Grad vs. Dennis Buzukja

Result: Submission (arm-triangle choke) at R2 4:33

Bogdan Grad set the tone early, taking Dennis Buzukja down and working him over against the fence before Buzukja scrambled back to his feet. The two traded in the center before Grad scored another takedown and hunted for a kimura, abandoning it for a guillotine attempt that Buzukja also survived. Round two saw Grad continue to chase takedowns while Buzukja started finding success on the feet, landing a right hook and a clean uppercut as Grad visibly began to slow down. It didn't matter in the end: Grad got one more takedown against the fence, locked in an arm-triangle choke, and referee Herzog waved it off when Buzukja appeared to tap.


In slow motion, it actually looks like Buzukja might not have been tapping at all, more like trying to reposition his leg, though the fight likely would have ended soon after regardless. Buzukja has every right to be upset about how that was called, and he'll probably get cut following what amounted to a "shit stain" of a performance either way, a tough break for the American. As for Grad, still not sure what to make of him. Still don't think he's very good.

Mateusz Rębecki vs. Kyle Prepolec

Result: TKO (punches) at R1 4:41

Mateusz Rębecki wasted no time, taking Kyle Prepolec down early and pinning him against the fence, where he ground him down before peppering him with punishing ground and pound until the referee had seen enough.


Prepolec just isn't UFC caliber at this point, it's time to move on from him. As for Rębecki, still not sure he's actually that good, but he was good enough to take out this particular can.

Nina Nikolija Milošević vs. Hailey Cowan

Result: TKO (punch to the body) at R1 3:41

Nina Nikolija Milošević pressed forward from the opening bell, working behind jabs and body kicks while Hailey Cowan's takedown attempts looked shaky at best and were repeatedly abandoned. A body shot from Milošević eventually buckled Cowan, who covered up in obvious pain and retreated to the cage. The referee let it continue, and Cowan tried to fight back before another body shot from Milošević ended it for good.


The referee probably should have stepped in sooner there. Cowan looked completely stunned and could have been hurt far worse than necessary. That said, Cowan really does look like she doesn't belong at this level, an embarrassing performance against a fighter making her own UFC debut. Not entirely sure what to make of Milošević yet off this one, but she's earned another fight in the UFC.

Jovan Leka vs. Alexander Poppeck

Result: TKO (body kick and punches) at R1 2:22

Jovan Leka controlled the center of the Octagon from the start while Alexander Poppeck stayed on the outside, and after a quiet exchange of jabs, Leka landed a body kick that put Poppeck on the canvas. Leka followed up with ground and pound, and that was that.


Poppeck probably should have stayed in Greece. That was a brutal first-round finish from the Serb. He just beat a guy off the regional scene, so it's hard to say exactly what to make of the performance overall, but that was a genuinely nasty kick.

Stephanie Luciano vs. Marina Spasić

Result: Submission (rear-naked choke) at R1 3:20

Stephanie Luciano wasted no time, scoring an early takedown on Marina Spasić and sinking in a rear-naked choke to get the tap before the round was even halfway over.


That was a complete mismatch from the jump. Spasić really only got this matchup because she's Serbian.
