Summary

Overall, a pretty shit card. Completely forgettable, from top to bottom. The main event was terrible, the prelims were mostly terrible, and there wasn't much worth remembering on the rest of the main card either.


The headliner between Magomed Ankalaev and Bogdan Guskov summed up the whole night. Guskov flat out did not want to fight, running away from Ankalaev for five rounds while Ankalaev tried to engage him like a professional, not an idiot. Neither man did anything to improve his case for a title shot. If anything, this is a really good opportunity for Paulo Costa to get an interim title shot later this year.


It got worse before it got better. Ramazan Temirov knocked out a badly declining Steve Erceg, looking completely juiced out of his mind doing it, just weeks removed from a PED suspension. Magomed Tuchalov somehow opened as a -1000 favorite over Brendson Ribeiro and still couldn't put him away, which says everything about how insane the odds were on this card. That's the Middle Eastern glaze running at full strength: the UFC clearly tried to stack this card with Middle Eastern fighters, and the result was a largely lackluster event.


Not everything was a disaster. Valter Walker got his sixth straight leg lock finish in a row, this time a calf slicer, and it remains baffling that fighters keep walking directly into it. Rizvan Kuniev put a brutal, one-sided beating on Tyrell Fortune that arguably should have been stopped well before the third round. And Sam Patterson finished Santiago Ponzinibbio, who really needs to hang it up at this point, in a reasonably entertaining fight given everything else going on around it.


Final Score: 6.3/10



Main Card

Magomed Ankalaev vs. Bogdan Guskov

Result: TKO (punches) at R5 2:41

This was supposed to settle something about the light heavyweight title picture. Instead it turned into a five-round exercise in one man refusing to fight. Magomed Ankalaev was lighter on his feet from the opening bell, using teep kicks to keep distance, switching stances fluidly, and mixing in body kicks and a head kick, but the overall output was low and the round was slow. Referee Mark Goddard spent much of the round warning Bogdan Guskov about sticking his fingers out toward Ankalaev's face.


The second round was more of the same, just with Ankalaev clipping Guskov and stinging him with a straight left while Guskov simply walked backward around the octagon without engaging. Ankalaev tried a takedown that Guskov denied, worked over-unders against the fence looking for a trip, and came up empty. Another extremely dull round. The third was worse: Guskov did nothing except walk backward, absorbing the occasional low kick and side kick to the body without offering anything back. By the fourth, Ankalaev had seen enough and went to the ground game, getting a single leg, working to half guard against the cage, landing ground and pound, and taking the back to hunt a rear naked choke and a neck crank, both of which Guskov turned away from. In the fifth, Ankalaev took him down again and simply wore on him until Goddard stepped in and waved it off in an anticlimactic finish, since Guskov wasn't fighting back at all.


Pretty shit main event. Guskov literally didn't want to fight, and Ankalaev tried to engage him the right way instead of chasing him down like an idiot, but Guskov just kept running. Neither fighter did anything here to improve his case for a title shot. If anything, this performance opens the door for Paulo Costa to get an interim title shot later this year.

Ramazan Temirov vs. Steve Erceg

Result: KO (punch) at R1 4:21

A spinning back kick to the body from Ramazan Temirov put Steve Erceg down early, though he popped right back up. Erceg answered with a takedown attempt and briefly got Temirov's back, but couldn't hold the position. Temirov kept spamming wild, unorthodox offense, and it took Erceg a while to settle the pace, working behind a steady jab and starting to walk Temirov down. Temirov clipped him again, Erceg shot in for a single leg and got back to his feet, but from there he was on the retreat. Temirov stalked him down, got him tied up against the cage, and flatlined him before the end of the first round.


Nasty finish from Temirov. Tough break for Erceg, who was on top of the world two years ago and is now stacking up losses in the flyweight division. As for Temirov, he is completely juiced out of his mind right now. He got suspended for PEDs last year, and that suspension only ended a couple of weeks before this fight.

Magomed Zaynukov vs. Damian Rzepecki

Result: Decision (unanimous) (30–27, 30–27, 30–27) at R3 5:00

Magomed Zaynukov ("John Pork") went to work early, stomping the leg and eventually holding Damian Rzepecki up against the cage, kneeing him to the thigh. They traded position control in the clinch, and Zaynukov turned Rzepecki around to knee him to the body. Then things got messy: Zaynukov landed an elbow to the head along with an eye poke, which drew a stern warning from Mark Goddard but no point deduction, despite badly poking Rzepecki in the eye and following it up with an elbow. Rzepecki got a takedown and took Zaynukov's back late in the round, and Zaynukov had to work back to his feet and look for a jumping knee to survive it.


Zaynukov opened the second with a couple of clean shots down the pipe. Rzepecki shot for a takedown, but Zaynukov turned it around and got on top instead. Rzepecki fought off the worst of it and got back up, and the fight went back into the clinch against the fence, where Rzepecki struggled to do damage before Zaynukov circled out. Zaynukov then hurt Rzepecki badly with an elbow and started teeing off, forcing Rzepecki into a desperation clinch to survive. Rzepecki answered with a spinning back kick to the body, but by the end of the round his face was bloodied and messed up.


Zaynukov started to visibly tire in the third, his output dropping and his mouth hanging open as he breathed heavily, but it didn't matter. Rzepecki kept looking for clinches and takedowns, even landing an elbow in the clinch and briefly getting a takedown in the center, but Zaynukov popped right back up every time and cruised to a clean sweep on the scorecards.


A dominant win for John Pork, though the dude needs to be careful about fouling people while he's out there. Rzepecki didn't look bad at all in defeat and should get another fight in the UFC off this performance.

Rizvan Kuniev vs. Tyrell Fortune

Result: TKO (knees) at R3 1:12

Rizvan Kuniev backed Tyrell Fortune up to the fence early and kneed him to the body, and from there it was a systematic beating. Kuniev walked Fortune down and battered him, got him into the clinch and hurt him, and kept walking him down and battering him some more. He hurt Fortune badly, dug to the body, and started head hunting, and somehow Fortune survived it, landing a lone uppercut in the clinch as his only real answer.


By the second round, Fortune's right eye was completely messed up. Kuniev kept eating him up against the cage while Fortune wasn't even fighting back anymore, just trying to hold Kuniev close to slow the damage, managing only a single clipping right hand in return. The third round was more of the same brutality until Kuniev landed a jumping knee to the head that put Fortune down, and the referee stopped it soon after.


It's genuinely unclear what was going on out there. The referee and Fortune's corner both had multiple opportunities to stop this fight before it ever reached the third round, and it never should have gotten there. Fortune said afterward that he had a broken rib and couldn't breathe. This was a one-sided beatdown from start to finish, but credit where it's due: Fortune has a hell of a chin.

Abubakar Vagaev vs. Saygid Izagakhmaev

Result: Decision (unanimous) (29–28, 29–28, 29–28) at R3 5:00

Abubakar Vagaev got an immediate takedown early, and the fight settled into a clear pattern: Saygid Izagakhmaev spent the first round mostly just trying to survive Vagaev's grappling. That trend continued into the second, and while Izagakhmaev did fight better in the third round, it was too little too late by that point.


This was a straightforward striker versus grappler fight, and Vagaev ultimately got the better of the exchanges across all three rounds.

Prelims

Valter Walker vs. Thomas Petersen

Result: Submission (calf slicer) at R1 1:32

Valter Walker threw a body kick up the middle that Thomas Petersen caught, and Walker immediately pulled guard to take the fight to the ground. From there Petersen found himself in Walker's guard, and Walker went hunting for a heel hook. Petersen tried to roll out of it, but Walker stayed attached and chained it into a calf slicer for the tap at 1:32 of the first round.


Wild. That's six leg lock finishes in a row now for Walker. With this win banked, he can push for a new contract and start fighting ranked opposition. It's genuinely bizarre that everyone in the sport knows this finish is coming and opponents still fall victim to it every time. As for Petersen, the real question is why he ever followed Walker to the ground at all. When Walker pulled guard, standing up and running away from the Brazilian was right there as an option.

Muhammad Saidov vs. Dustin Jacoby

Result: TKO (head kick and punches) at R2 4:49

Muhammad Saidov opened with calf kicks and clocked Dustin Jacoby clean with a left, and Jacoby struggled to get any offense going from there, managing only the occasional body kick. Jacoby leaned on low kicks as his primary weapon in the second, but Saidov surged forward, clipped him with a right hook, then unloaded a spinning body kick before landing a nasty head kick that had Jacoby in trouble. Saidov just kept pressuring and hammering him from there until the fight was stopped.


A nasty finish from Saidov. Jacoby simply did not look good out there, and this is one of those performances that makes you question whether he should still be in the UFC.

Sam Patterson vs. Santiago Ponzinibbio

Result: TKO (punches) at R2 3:06

Santiago Ponzinibbio cracked Sam Patterson with a right hand early, and Patterson recovered and went back to work, though he still seemed hesitant to pull the trigger. Ponzinibbio had clearly retreated into fighting off the back foot, and Patterson started cracking him with increasing frequency, even dropping him momentarily before the round ended.


By the second round, Patterson had found his rhythm completely, moving in to tag Ponzinibbio and retreating out cleanly and easily. He trapped Ponzinibbio against the fence and landed some nice punches, then dropped him and followed him down to add ground and pound. Patterson let him back up, only to finish the fight moments later.


A rough start from Patterson, but he looked good by the end. Ponzinibbio has got to hang it up at this point. Given everything else on this card, this was a reasonably entertaining fight. Patterson also called out the winner of Medic vs. Rodriguez afterward, and that's a good callout, that's the fight to make for him next.

Axel Sola vs. Ismael Bonfim

Result: Submission (brabo choke) at R1 4:44

Ismael Bonfim shot in early for a takedown and got Axel Sola up against the cage, burning all of his energy just trying to hold him there. Sola reversed the position, locked in a d'arce choke, and got the tap. Fight over.


Bonfim ain't got it. A great win for Sola, who deserves a ranked opponent next, or at the very least someone just outside the rankings.

Magomed Tuchalov vs. Brendson Ribeiro

Result: Decision (unanimous) (29–28, 29–28, 29–28) at R3 5:00

The first round was mostly a feeling-out process on the feet. Brendson Ribeiro caught Magomed Tuchalov up against the fence, but Tuchalov circled away easily. For most of the round, Ribeiro was the only one actually trying to fight, and every time he tried to engage, Tuchalov just circled away instead. Ribeiro managed to sting him, and the two cracked each other right at the bell.


Tuchalov came out for the second with a cut under his left eye already, throwing calf kicks and trying a single leg that Ribeiro shucked off. He stung Ribeiro with a right hand and got a takedown, though the action briefly stopped for an eye poke from Tuchalov. Ribeiro got back on the pressure, walking Tuchalov down, but Tuchalov cracked him again with a right and followed it with another takedown, still without finding the finish. Ribeiro kept walking Tuchalov down to start the third, and Tuchalov leaned on kicks again. Ribeiro clipped him, but Tuchalov answered with a takedown, though he couldn't pass Ribeiro's guard and had to stand back up and let him back to his feet. Ribeiro tried a takedown of his own, Tuchalov threw him back to the ground, and Ribeiro used the fence to climb back up before Tuchalov dragged him back down again, worked to the back, scrambled into side control, and just wore on him the rest of the way.


It's insane that Tuchalov was a -1000 favorite going into this fight. Another absolute dud in Abu Dhabi. Ribeiro sucks, so this really wasn't a great debut for Tuchalov despite the win.

Nurullo Aliev vs. Mike Davis

Result: Decision (unanimous) (30–27, 30–27, 30–27) at R3 5:00

Nurullo Aliev shot in for a double leg early and held Mike Davis against the fence before finishing the takedown. Davis got back to his feet and the two clinched, but Aliev shot in again and put him right back down, wearing on him against the fence, getting a hook in, and landing ground and pound. Davis scrambled, ended up on his back again, and Aliev continued wearing on him from the back, mixing in knees to the thigh. Davis found an elbow from the bottom, got up, and briefly took Aliev's back, but Aliev worked free, got back into guard, and landed a hammerfist. Davis threw up a triangle from the bottom as the round ended.


Aliev got Davis right back down early in the second and kept wearing on him, mostly just laying on him in half guard without doing much damage. Davis got to the fence and back to his base, but Aliev stayed glued to him, eventually landing enough ground and pound that the referee had to warn him about the back of the head. Davis got up and Aliev tagged him on the break. Davis actually landed a nice 1-2 on the feet, but Aliev shot right back in for another takedown, added knees to the thigh, and just continued holding him against the fence through the closing seconds.


Davis came out more active in the third, clearly aware he needed a finish, but Aliev shot in for another single leg and got it in the center of the octagon, settling into a straight lay and pray. Davis got up, only to eat a knee to the head and get taken down again, and Aliev just kept wearing on him the rest of the way to a clean sweep on the cards.


Boring as fuck. Davis needs to do a much better job defending takedowns and getting back up if he wants to hang with the big boys. Aliev, for his part, needs to mix in more of his actual game: more ground and pound, more submission attempts, anything. What we saw from Aliev tonight will not get him into the lightweight top 10.

Abdul Hussein vs. Cody Gibson

Result: Submission (arm-triangle choke) at R3 3:07

Cody Gibson was active early, working the outside with jabs and leg kicks before getting a takedown and bringing Abdul Hussein down. He worked into a mounted guillotine and transitioned it into an anaconda choke, but Hussein survived, escaped, and ended up on top. The two scrambled and Gibson got back to his feet, and they clinched against the cage before exchanging in the center to close out the round.


Hussein took over in the second, getting Gibson to the ground and wearing on him against the cage. He threw up a guillotine that Gibson escaped, then moved into side control, mostly just holding Gibson there without advancing position or doing real damage. Hussein landed some ground and pound and tried an anaconda choke before abandoning it. Gibson scrambled away from the fence and started wearing on Hussein in the center of the octagon, and Hussein went for another crank-type choke, only to abandon that one too.


Gibson dug to the body to start the third, and Hussein separated and backed him up against the fence, starting to tee off. Gibson clipped him and Hussein went down, and Gibson followed him to the ground and reversed position. But Hussein started wearing on Gibson again, and when Gibson got back to his feet, Hussein simply threw him back down, sank in an arm triangle, and got the tap.


Terrible fight. Gibson was already done after the first round, and it took Hussein way too long to actually finish it. Cut them both.
