Overview

UFC Abu Dhabi is headlined by a real title-picture fight, but a late scratch already reshaped the card before a single punch was thrown. Islam Dulatov's welterweight bout against Wellington Turman was pulled just hours before the event after Dulatov fell ill, so the undefeated prospect's showcase is off, and the main card drops to five fights.

The headliner still delivers plenty of intrigue. Magomed Ankalaev makes his first walk since losing the light heavyweight title to Alex Pereira last October, and he does it against a short-notice replacement in Bogdan Guskov, who stepped in for an injured Khalil Rountree Jr. and has already declared that a win means an automatic title shot. Ankalaev is the heavy favorite, but a former champion coming off a stoppage loss on a five-month turnaround against a finisher with a 100 percent UFC finish rate is not a formality.

The rest of the main card is loaded with must-see stylistic clashes and prospect debuts. Steve Erceg tries to keep climbing back up the flyweight ladder against a returning-from-suspension Ramazan Temirov. Magomed Zaynukov and Damian Rzepecki, two unbeaten prospects, collide in each other's UFC debuts. Rizvan Kuniev and Tyrell Fortune clash in a ranked heavyweight fight. And Abubakar Vagaev faces Saygid Izagakhmaev in a Chimaev-camp-vs-Makhachev-camp welterweight opener.

The prelims bring their own storylines: Valter Walker defends the division's longest active win streak, all four wins by heel hook, against Thomas Petersen. Santiago Ponzinibbio returns from over a year off to face Sam Patterson. And Ismael Bonfim, who infamously collapsed on the scale missing weight in his last outing, looks to save his roster spot against unbeaten prospect Axel Sola.


Main Card Picks

Magomed Ankalaev vs. Bogdan Guskov

Magomed Ankalaev (20-2-1) is the former light heavyweight champion and current No. 1 contender, making his first appearance since losing the title back to Alex Pereira via TKO last October. Before that stoppage loss, his resume included a unanimous decision win over Pereira to claim the title in the first place, plus wins over Aleksandar Rakić and Johnny Walker. He's a heavy favorite here at around -500, though he has struggled to capture fan and promotional interest despite the ranking and the title reign, and it's unclear whether even a dominant win secures him an immediate rematch.

Bogdan Guskov (18-3-1) is the No. 9-ranked short-notice replacement, stepping in after Khalil Rountree Jr. withdrew injured. He owns a 100 percent finish rate across his UFC wins, three of them by knockout, most recently a KO of Nikita Krylov before a majority draw with Jan Błachowicz. He's an underdog around +375, but he isn't hiding his intentions: a win here means he's "first to fight for the title, 100 percent."

Pick: Magomed Ankalaev

Method: Decision

Ankalaev probably doesn't have the striking power or submission ability to finish Bogdan Guskov outright, but he wins this at the end regardless. Ankalaev's volume kickboxing and pressure over five rounds should be enough to out-point a fighter who is walking in on short notice, even one with real knockout power. Guskov has finished three of his five UFC wins by knockout, so there's always a puncher's chance, but the gap in overall craft and championship-level experience gets Ankalaev the decision.

Steve Erceg vs. Ramazan Temirov

Steve Erceg (14-4) was fast-tracked to a title shot against then-champion Alexandre Pantoja, a fight that exposed some durability and pace issues against elite pressure. He then dropped three straight (Pantoja, Kai Kara-France, Brandon Moreno) before steadying himself with back-to-back decision wins, most recently over Tim Elliott in his home city of Perth. He's a slight favorite here around -136.

Ramazan Temirov ("Temurlan") is an orthodox Uzbek finisher who joined the UFC in 2024 with a perfect 2-0 start, a first-round KO of CJ Vergara in his debut followed by a unanimous decision over Charles Johnson. He returns from a PED suspension that followed that Johnson win, which is the headline storyline heading into this one. He's an underdog around +106.

Pick: Steve Erceg

Method: Decision

Ramazan Temirov is getting a serious bump up in competition here, even though he doesn't have any truly significant wins on his UFC ledger yet. That's exactly the kind of opportunity Steve Erceg needs to rise to. Erceg has already been to the deep end against a real champion in Pantoja, and that experience, combined with his jab, straight right, and grappling if he needs to slow things down, should be enough to see him through a decision.

Magomed Zaynukov vs. Damian Rzepecki

Magomed Zaynukov ("Chanco," also known online as "Wild" or by the meme nickname "John Pork") is 8-0, a decorated Muay Thai fighter making his long-awaited UFC debut after securing his contract on the Contender Series. He's known as a slick, high-pace striker who can also wrestle, and he's a slight favorite here around -325.

Damian Rzepecki ("The Butcher") is 10-0, the former FEN lightweight champion and one of the rare prospects signed directly to the UFC without going through Contender Series. He's an all-action, high-volume pressure fighter with 9 finishes in 10 wins, 5 by submission and 4 by knockout. He's an underdog around +260.

Pick: Magomed Zaynukov

Method: Decision

This is a pure prospect-vs-prospect debut showcase, two unbeaten fighters meeting in each other's UFC debuts. Magomed Zaynukov has good striking on the feet and solid grappling after training with Team Khabib, and that well-rounded package should be enough to point his way to a decision victory over Damian Rzepecki, whose pressure-heavy style will get him in the fight early but shouldn't be enough to keep Zaynukov off him for three rounds.

Rizvan Kuniev vs. Tyrell Fortune

Rizvan Kuniev (13-3-1) is the No. 8-ranked heavyweight who has methodically climbed the division, coming off a signature decision win over elite grappler Jailton Almeida after a split decision loss to Curtis Blaydes. He's versatile on the feet and on the ground, though his defense isn't airtight, and he's the favorite here around -325 to -335.

Tyrell Fortune (18-3) is a former NCAA Division II wrestling champion with 11 of his 18 wins by knockout, 1-0 in the UFC after debuting with a decision win over veteran Marcin Tybura in March. He's an underdog around +260.

Pick: Rizvan Kuniev

Method: KO/TKO

No real ambiguity here. Rizvan Kuniev just came off a win over Jailton Almeida and fought Curtis Blaydes right before that, a level of heavyweight competition Tyrell Fortune simply hasn't been tested against yet. Fortune is not on Kuniev's level, and he gets leveled quickly in this one, first or second round.

Abubakar Vagaev vs. Saygid Izagakhmaev

Abubakar Vagaev (24-4) is a 33-year-old Chechen making his UFC debut after a decade-plus pro career, with notable wins over Michel Pereira, Luiz Dutra Jr., Michel Prazeres, and Albert Tumenov. He's a former ACA (Absolute Championship Akhmat) champion, an orthodox striker who likes distance and explosive bursts punctuated by leg kicks, and a training partner of Khamzat Chimaev.

Saygid Izagakhmaev (22-3) is a UFC sophomore who debuted last November in Doha with a split decision loss to Nicolas Dalby that snapped a five-fight win streak. He's a Combat Sambo specialist out of the Team Khabib camp who excels with takedowns and can finish on the feet or the canvas. He replaced Magomedrasul Gasanov on short notice, sliding this fight down to 170 pounds.

Pick: Saygid Izagakhmaev

Method: Decision

The loss to Nicolas Dalby is more impressive than anything Abubakar Vagaev has done in his career to date, even in defeat. There's too much hype around Vagaev right now purely because of his connection to Chimaev's camp, and if he weren't training there, the odds would be in Saygid Izagakhmaev's favor. Izagakhmaev's sambo-based takedown and finishing threat should be enough to get the job done here on the scorecards.

Prelims Picks

Valter Walker vs. Thomas Petersen

Valter Walker ("The Clean Monster") is 15-1, the No. 13-ranked heavyweight riding the division's longest active win streak at four, and every one of those wins has come by heel hook. He's the favorite here around -180.

Thomas Petersen ("The Train") is 11-4, standing 6'1" and weighing 250 pounds. He's an underdog around +155.

Pick: Valter Walker

Method: Submission

Valter Walker is on a crazy heel hook submission roll, and it isn't stopping tonight. All four of his UFC wins have come by the exact same finish, and there's no reason to expect Thomas Petersen to be the man who solves it. The Brazilian finishes this fight in the first round with a signature heel hook.

Dustin Jacoby vs. Muhammad Saidov

Dustin Jacoby ("The Hanyak") is 22-9-1, a veteran with over a dozen UFC appearances dating back to 2011, currently on a three-fight finishing streak. He brings a rangy kickboxing pedigree and enters as the bigger, more experienced fighter.

Muhammad Saidov is 9-0, a promotional newcomer making his UFC debut after his second UAE Warriors finish earned him a contract. He carries a perfect record with 8 finishes in 9 wins.

Pick: Dustin Jacoby

Method: Decision

Dustin Jacoby is good and has good striking, full stop. Against a debuting Muhammad Saidov, that experience edge and technical striking pedigree should let him point his way to a decision here.

Santiago Ponzinibbio vs. Sam Patterson

Santiago Ponzinibbio is a 39-year-old Argentine veteran, once a top welterweight contender, now 2-5 across his last seven fights and hasn't fought since May 2025. He says this comeback isn't about the money, it's about the challenge.

Sam Patterson (14-3-1) is coming off a lackluster unanimous decision loss to Michael Page in March. He carries an unusually high stoppage rate for his career, 93 percent per Sherdog.

Pick: Sam Patterson

Method: KO/TKO

After a weird performance against MVP earlier this year, Sam Patterson is back, and a lot of the blame for that showing should fall on Michael Page's fight style rather than on Patterson himself. Page basically just wants to counter and catch you off the back foot, and Patterson wisely didn't want to play into that game and get put to sleep. Santiago Ponzinibbio is over the hill at this point, and he gets knocked out early in this one.

Ismael Bonfim vs. Axel Sola

Ismael Bonfim ("Marreta") is 20-6, back against the wall after back-to-back losses to Nazim Sadykhov and Chris Padilla in 2025, the latter after missing weight by five pounds and collapsing on the scale. He returns here after an eight-month layoff, an underdog around +165.

Axel Sola is 11-0-1, an unbeaten prospect with 6 KOs and 1 submission among his wins, who says the reward is better if he puts on a show. He's the favorite around -200.

Pick: Axel Sola

Method: Decision

Axel Sola is still a big guy with power in his hands. On top of that, he's coming off a standout performance against Mason Jones and should get the job done again this weekend. Ismael Bonfim hasn't looked good in his last several performances, and the weigh-in disaster only adds to the concerns heading into this one.

Magomed Tuchalov vs. Brendson Ribeiro

Magomed Tuchalov is 6-0, a Dagestani fighter making his UFC debut, 6'5", with a habit of quickly dispatching opponents. Bookmakers see him as a huge favorite here.

Brendson Ribeiro is 17-10, a Brazilian veteran on a three-fight losing streak, fighting to save his roster spot.

Pick: Magomed Tuchalov

Method: KO/TKO

Magomed Tuchalov is making his debut here, but that shouldn't matter against this level of opposition. Brendson Ribeiro is really trash at this point in his career. He will not only lose this fight, he'll get put to sleep in the process.

Nurullo Aliev vs. Mike Davis

Nurullo Aliev ("Tajik Eagle") is 11-0, a wrestling-heavy rising contender in the lightweight division. He's the favorite here around -205.

Mike Davis ("Beast Boy") is 12-3, a 33-year-old out of Orlando returning after nearly a year off. He says he's ready to reward UFC patience with a statement performance and that opponents "won't overlook him anymore." He's an underdog around +170.

Pick: Mike Davis

Method: Decision

Mike Davis has significantly more experience in the UFC, and that should favor him heavily in this matchup. Nurullo Aliev's two most recent wins have come against scrubs, so his unbeaten record still has to justify itself against real competition at this point in his career, and this is that test.

Cody Gibson vs. Abdul Hussein

Cody Gibson is a 38-year-old veteran (21-12) stepping in on short notice to save this fight after Hussein's original opponent, Muin Gafurov, got injured.

Abdul Hussein is a Finnish fighter (15-2) making his UFC debut with a perfect 5-0 record under UAE Warriors, most recently knocking out former UFC prospect Tyson Nam to win the ICF title.

Pick: Abdul Hussein

Method: KO/TKO

Cody Gibson isn't UFC caliber at this point and is super chinny. This will be his last walk in the UFC, and he gets put to sleep early by Abdul Hussein.
