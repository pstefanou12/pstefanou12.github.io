Summary

UFC 329 was supposed to be the biggest card of the year, and instead it will be remembered as the night the Conor McGregor business officially died. Scores of 8 and above are reserved for cards that have something special about them, and a McGregor card had the potential to be a 9 or higher. It did not deliver. Everything besides the main event earned a 7.9, which is about the ceiling for a card of that caliber, but the main event was a 0/10. Average the two together and you get 3.95. That is the math on a night where the co-stars showed up and the headliner fell apart, literally.


The main event was a scam. Conor McGregor threw a kick, collapsed to the canvas, ate some ground and pound from Max Holloway, and the referee waved it off because something was wrong with his knee. Sixty-nine seconds, no media week, no fight. That has to be the last time McGregor fights. The last seven years of his career have been failed comebacks stacked on failed comebacks, and you simply cannot sell another one. Watching it genuinely felt like watching someone whose best days are behind him and who refuses to admit it. It's just sad, man.


The co-main somehow managed to be almost as absurd. Benoit Saint Denis shot a takedown into the one submission everyone on the planet knew Paddy Pimblett was waiting for, and got choked unconscious in under a minute. BSD continues to get fraud checked in the UFC, and Paddy the Baddy is an absolute stud. The Paddy vs. Ilia fight is sitting right there on a silver platter: book it, winner gets the next crack at the title after Arman.


Mario Bautista took a decision over Cory Sandhagen that I scored the other way, though it was close enough to go either direction. Elsewhere, Robert Whittaker announced that Bobby Knuckles is back by breaking Nikita Krylov's jaw, King Green somehow got it done again at 39 with a body shot finish of a Terrance McKinney who was seconds away from a 10-8 round, and Brandon Royval and Lone'er Kavanagh put on the fight of the night in a flyweight war.


One more thing: a lot of people are giving Gable Steveson too much credit for his win. You cannot be getting hit by scrubs at heavyweight. If Elisha Ellison is touching you, a real contender will eat you alive.


Final Score: 3.95/10



Main Card

Max Holloway vs. Conor McGregor

Result: TKO (knee injury) at R1 1:09

Two years of buildup, a Sphere-sized promotional machine, and the long-awaited rematch of one of the most famous debuts in UFC history. It lasted 69 seconds. Conor McGregor threw a kick early, fell to the canvas, and Max Holloway jumped on him with ground and pound. McGregor made it back to his feet, but the referee stopped the fight immediately because something was wrong with his knee. That was it. That was the whole main event.


What a scam. What a terrible fight. McGregor did not do a single piece of media all week, showed up, and broke down inside of a round. He has lost serious credibility, and this has to be the last time he fights. Fans will not buy tickets to see McGregor again, and honestly, his body might not even allow it. Think about the actual timeline here: he fought Mayweather in 2017 and lost. Came back to challenge Khabib for the lightweight title in 2018 and lost. Came back in 2020 to beat Cerrone, then got knocked out by Poirier in 2021. They could sell the second Poirier fight because he held a win over him, and then his leg snapped in half in the trilogy. The big 2024 comeback against Chandler evaporated weeks before the fight. Now this was supposed to be the real comeback, two years later, and he loses by fluke injury. The last seven years of his career have been nothing but comebacks, and none of them have been successful. You cannot sell another one.


This fight genuinely felt like watching someone whose best days are behind him and who refuses to admit it. It's just sad, man. And I don't think it's an age thing necessarily, it's a take care of yourself thing. Tell your kids: if you want legitimate longevity in your life, you need to take care of yourself. As for the fallout, the party's over. Run Paddy Pimblett vs. Ilia, and Max is probably going to have to fight Ruffy next.

Paddy Pimblett vs. Benoît Saint Denis

Result: Technical Submission (Peruvian necktie) at R1 0:52

Everyone on X spent fight week saying the same thing: the one glaring hole in Benoit Saint Denis's game is that he gives up his neck on takedowns, and Paddy Pimblett is exactly the guy who makes you pay for it with guillotines and d'arces. So what did BSD do? He shot a takedown in the opening seconds, stuck his neck directly into Paddy's arms, and got choked unconscious 52 seconds into the fight. Unbelievable.


Did your team not prep you for this? You deserve to lose. BSD really does suck, and he continues to get fraud checked every time he steps up in the UFC. Handing the one submission your opponent is world class at to him on a silver platter is inexcusable at this level.


Paddy the Baddy, meanwhile, is unreal. What an absolute stud. Another title shot could be in his future, and real talk, the Paddy vs. Ilia fight is now sitting right there on a silver platter. Time to book it, with the winner getting the next crack at the title after Arman.

Mario Bautista vs. Cory Sandhagen

Result: Decision (unanimous) (29–28, 29–28, 29–28) at R3 5:00

This one had real stakes in the bantamweight title picture, and it delivered a solid, if uneven, fight. Cory Sandhagen came out sharp and clipped Mario Bautista early, hurting him in the opening minutes. Bautista tried to answer with a body lock takedown that Sandhagen stuffed, and Sandhagen even reversed into his own clinch against the cage. But Bautista settled in, started putting combinations together in the center, and mixed in a takedown off his boxing. He hunted a Suloev stretch late in the round, and while Sandhagen survived the submission, he limped back to his corner with a compromised leg.


Sandhagen actually looked better in the second, even as Bautista went straight after the bad leg to open the round. The problem was that Bautista was clearly tiring, and the output from both men dropped significantly in a lackluster middle frame. Then the third turned the fight back on: Bautista dropped Sandhagen with a left hook, followed him to the ground, and poured on ground and pound while Sandhagen used his guard to weather the storm. When Sandhagen got up, Bautista hurt him again with uppercuts on the break, took his back off another takedown, and spent the closing stretch dragging Sandhagen around the octagon with body lock after body lock.


Damn, I thought Cory won that fight. It was close and could have gone either way, but the judges gave Bautista all three rounds on all three cards. The first and third rounds were entertaining, the second was forgettable, but overall a solid fight. Give Bautista one more and then he gets a title shot. The division math is genuinely tricky: if Yan wins his next fight, give him Merab. If Merab wins, give Umar the title shot, run O'Malley vs. Yan, and give Bautista Talbott.

Brandon Royval vs. Lone'er Kavanagh

Result: Submission (rear-naked choke) at R3 3:40

Great fight, and the best pure action of the night. Brandon Royval spent the first round stalking Lone'er Kavanagh down, bouncing a head kick off his guard, digging inside low kicks, and pumping the jab before shooting a takedown and getting Kavanagh to the canvas. Royval worked to side control and punished Kavanagh with ground and pound until the Brit finally worked his way back up. The second round was where things got dicey: Kavanagh cracked Royval with a big right hook and put him on the floor, followed him down with ground and pound, and later wobbled him with heavy elbows on the break. Royval kept doing what Royval does, answering with pressure, jabs, takedown attempts, and a pace that started to outwork Kavanagh down the stretch.


The third settled it. Royval stung Kavanagh early, and when Kavanagh jumped a guillotine off a takedown attempt, he wound up on the wrong end of a nightmare sequence: Royval landed big elbows from top position, locked up an arm triangle that Kavanagh somehow survived, then took the back when Kavanagh tried to stand. The rear naked choke followed, and Kavanagh had to tap at 3:40 of round three.


Flyweight is probably the best and most entertaining division in the sport right now. The guys are the most technical, they can knock you out or submit you, and they have the cardio to make five rounds entertaining. Get the flyweights out of the Apex. As for the takeaways: Kavanagh has knockout power, but not much else right now, and needs serious work on his cardio and grappling. And Royval's chin is officially suspect. He got dropped again, and at this point in his career, that is the thing that will hold him back.

King Green vs. Terrance McKinney

Result: TKO (punches) at R1 4:59

Terrance McKinney spent four minutes and fifty seconds winning this fight, and nine seconds losing it. He clipped King Green with a right hand early, bloodied Bobby's face, landed a big left, and dug hard to the body. Then he got a takedown, mounted Green, flattened him out, and rained down ground and pound while threatening rear naked chokes over and over. Green was stuck punching backwards from a terrible position with McKinney on his back. This was a 10-8 round in progress, no question about it.


Then Bobby scrambled back to his feet, landed one body shot, and McKinney wilted and was finished right on the bell. Embarrassing stuff from McKinney. Literally on the way to a 10-8 round, and then he folds to a single body shot. McKinney is exciting, but he just isn't good, and this is the kind of collapse that proves it.


Bob Green, meanwhile, still somehow gets it done at 39 years old, and is now on a four fight win streak. Wild.

Prelims

Robert Whittaker vs. Nikita Krylov

Result: TKO (jaw injury) at R3 1:01

Nikita Krylov actually made the first round uncomfortable for Robert Whittaker. He pushed Whittaker to the cage, ground away in the clinch, got in deep on a single leg, and eventually got Whittaker down, wearing on him from the back and holding him on one knee for a long stretch. Whittaker had to fight out of a headlock just to get back to open space, though he closed the round well with crisp 1-2s and a head kick at the bell.


From the second round on, it was the Bobby Knuckles show. Whittaker stuffed Krylov's takedown, started landing jabs at will, and cracked him with a right hook against the cage that had Krylov on the move. Every time Krylov threw back, Whittaker stood his ground and answered. One jab snapped Krylov's head back so hard it looked like a knockdown waiting to happen. In the third, Krylov was hurt badly and retreating, and Whittaker put the finishing touch on it: a right hand that broke Krylov's jaw and forced the TKO stoppage at 1:01 of round three.


Bobby Knuckles is back, baby! That said, let's keep it in perspective: Krylov really just isn't that good, and whenever he fights anyone in the division with a real level of skill, he typically loses. It's also worth noting that Whittaker's hands were super low all fight, and it didn't feel like he carries the devastating knockout power that other guys in the division have. A fight with someone like Oezdemir makes sense next.

Gable Steveson vs. Elisha Ellison

Result: KO (knees and punches) at R1 2:31

An absolute slop fest out there. Gable Steveson got clipped early by Elisha Ellison, then threw a kick, slipped, and fell to the canvas before bouncing back up. His first takedown attempt got stuffed, with Ellison even threatening a guillotine off of it before abandoning it against the cage. Then came a groin shot from Steveson that stopped the action, which looked a little controversial, though Marc Goddard insisted he could hear it. When things restarted, Steveson landed a left hook that put Ellison down, but Ellison survived and hurt Steveson right back. Eventually Steveson cracked Ellison up against the cage, put him down, and followed with a shot to the downed opponent to end it.


Pretty pathetic stuff from a guy who is supposed to be the next coming at heavyweight. Steveson just charged forward eating shots the whole time and basically gave Ellison a chance in a fight he should have had zero business in. There is way too much glaze on Steveson right now. He looked really bad out there, and I think he loses to nearly every single ranked heavyweight opponent in the division. You cannot be getting hit clean by scrubs at heavyweight; if Elisha Ellison is touching you, a real contender decapitates you.


The path forward is obvious: keep feeding Steveson multiple unranked opponents so he doesn't get his head taken off by a real heavyweight contender before he's ready.

Adrian Yañez vs. Cody Garbrandt

Result: TKO (punches) at R1 2:47

Cody Garbrandt came out like a man trying to end the night early, charging Adrian Yañez off the opening bell, pinning him against the cage, and swinging for the finish. Yañez survived the storm and circled away, and from that moment the fight flipped. They traded in the center, Yañez stung Garbrandt with a right hand that sent him retreating, and after Garbrandt half-heartedly looked for a takedown and abandoned it, Yañez strung together the combination that ended it. Garbrandt was done, and Herb Dean stopped the fight at 2:47 of the first.


Garbrandt has a glass chin. Brutal. He was extremely aggressive early, but the second Yañez settled in and started landing, it was over. Garbrandt took his gloves off in the cage after the fight, so the question has to be asked: is it over? Tough night at the office either way.


For Yañez, this is a great bounce back win after getting robbed against Ricky Simon in March. He beat Simon in everyone's eyes but the judges', and now he just slimed out Garbrandt in a round. Give him another fight up the rankings.

Luke Riley vs. Kai Kamaka III

Result: TKO (knees and punches) at R1 3:03

For a few minutes, Kai Kamaka III was actually landing the smarter shots. He went to work on Luke Riley's lead leg with low kicks as Riley leaned forward behind his jab, and he was doing a really good job countering in the exchanges while eating up that front leg. Riley invested in a left hook to the body, and then found the shot that mattered: a right hand that stung Kamaka and put him down. Kamaka made it back to his feet, but Riley pounced and battered him against the fence until the referee stepped in at 3:03 of the first.


Unfortunately, Kamaka is just not UFC caliber. Riley has good boxing, but there are still real gaps in his game that veterans in this division will eat up: he puts a ton of weight on his front leg and fights super flat-footed. Kamaka was already chewing that lead leg up before he got caught, and a good kickboxer would absolutely feast on it.

Early Prelims

Wang Cong vs. Tracy Cortez

Result: Decision (unanimous) (29–27, 29–27, 29–27) at R3 5:00

Tracy Cortez came out aggressive early, but Wang Cong was countering beautifully off the back foot, and it took about two minutes for the fight to turn into a slow-motion horror show. A low kick put Cortez on the ground, and when she got up she was clearly startled. Her lead leg was wrecked almost immediately, and somehow she never even switched her stance while it got abused for the rest of the round. Her takedown attempts went nowhere, with Wang using the fence to stay upright and slipping out of a guillotine attempt with ease.


By the second, Cortez's left leg gave out on her mid-takedown attempt and she collapsed to the canvas and pulled guard. She spent the rest of the round running away on a leg that might have been broken, surging forward in desperation while Wang circled and countered at will. Wang did lose a point for an illegal knee to the head of a downed Cortez, which paused the fight for a minute, but it changed nothing. In the third, a left hook completely shut Cortez's left eye, another leg kick left her barely able to walk, and when she finally tried switching stances she showed she can't fight southpaw at all. Wang just teed off on her down the stretch, tagging her at will.


An absolutely embarrassing performance and game plan from Cortez. She got the absolute shit beaten out of her for three rounds. How was the game plan not to go out there and risk takedowns at all costs? She can't switch stances, and she never had a chance of beating a former muay thai fighter on the feet. The good news for Cortez is that there will always be a career in OnlyFans waiting for her at the end of the day, but if she wants to keep fighting in the UFC, something needs to change, because that was terrible.


As for Wang, she probably needs one more fight before challenging Valentina Shevchenko, and regardless, I don't think she beats her. Valentina will just out-grapple and point-fight her way to a decision over the Chinese striker.

Damian Pinas vs. César Almeida

Result: KO (punch) at R1 4:44

César Almeida was putting on a striking clinic for four and a half minutes. He opened up with kicks at every level, calf kicks, body kicks, and kept digging to the body of Damian Pinas throughout the round. He even mixed in a takedown against the fence with a body lock before they separated back to the center. Pinas, meanwhile, looked completely lost. His output dropped off a cliff, he couldn't find a counter, and he was catching air with everything he threw. It looked like he simply didn't know what to do with Almeida on the feet.


Then, with sixteen seconds left in the round, Pinas found a counter right hand on the chin and Almeida went down, and that was it. Fight over at 4:44 of the first.


Let's be honest about what happened: Pinas was getting absolutely styled on until he landed one punch. He lacks technique, and he won't be able to rely on raw power forever. A good well-rounded fighter, or a Chimaev type, gets this done easily. He's just a dude who relies heavily on gear and genetics, and he will get fraud checked soon. As for Almeida, that's two tough losses in a row at 38 years old. I don't know what's next for him. Perhaps retirement.

Farid Basharat vs. John Garza

Result: Decision (unanimous) (30–27, 30–27, 29–28) at R3 5:00

Farid Basharat looked super poised early, controlling the octagon and picking his shots at will while John Garza bounced around looking like the nervous short-notice guy he was. Basharat got a double leg in the center, jumped to the back almost immediately, and locked in a body triangle, but Garza worked out of it and got back to his feet. Basharat stayed on the wrestling, dragged him back down, and moved to side control before Garza shrimped away again. From there, though, Garza started to look more confident, and even as he ate a head kick, a straight left, and a spinning elbow, he began stuffing takedowns and firing back.


The second round is where the fight changed shape. Basharat kept shooting and Garza kept scrambling to the fence to deny him, landing knees to the body in the clinch and walking forward with combinations. Basharat did complete another takedown into side control, but by the end of the round he was running around the octagon while Garza walked him down and teed off with Basharat barely defending. Basharat stole the ending with a late takedown and a right hand on the ground and pound. The third belonged mostly to Garza: Basharat was clearly tired, couldn't secure takedowns anymore, and got countered badly every time he came forward, eating right hooks, uppercuts, and body kicks. A big right from Basharat stung Garza late, and he closed with a takedown into side control, but he was mostly just laying on Garza, unable to land any real ground and pound, and Garza escaped his crucifix attempt easily.


Some fair context for Basharat: he took this fight on a couple of weeks' notice against a guy with barely any film to study, which makes gameplanning nearly impossible, and he reportedly went in with a serious injury. But he didn't look good. He couldn't get his takedowns going and never had a serious moment on the feet. He called out for a top 10-12 opponent afterward, and he probably deserves it, since he was scheduled to fight Ewing before taking this one on short notice. As for Garza, he deserves a Contender Series spot at the very least. Depending on the damage he took, give him another UFC fight with a full camp and an opponent closer to his experience level.

Ryan Gandra vs. Zachary Reese

Result: TKO (punches) at R1 1:15

This one didn't last long enough to learn much. Zachary Reese opened with a left hand and a body kick, and Ryan Gandra answered with a left of his own before stinging Reese with a right hand. Gandra was constantly switching stances, giving Reese different looks, and it took him all of 75 seconds to find the left hand that put Reese down. He followed up with ground and pound and Mark Beltran stopped the fight.


Zachary Reese is fucking dogshit. He's terrible. He had no chance tonight and he showed it. And no, this probably wasn't an early stoppage: Reese was wobbly when he got back to his feet, he was out of it.


It's tough to tell if Gandra is actually any good off of this, because beating Reese proves nothing. Maybe we'll find out in his next matchup.

Alessandro Costa vs. Cody Durden

Result: Submission (rear-naked choke) at R2 2:19

Alessandro Costa took this fight on two weeks' notice and still made it look like a mismatch. He spent the first round moving forward and stalking Cody Durden around the octagon, putting together a nasty combination against the cage and spamming spinning back kicks. To be fair, Durden was getting slightly ahead on the striking, since Costa kept exploding forward and landing without capitalizing enough, but Costa still landed a nice knee and another clean combination in the center, and shrugged off Durden's clinch attempt.


Durden tried to level change and mix in the grappling to start the second, and it backfired completely. The fight hit the ground with Costa in Durden's guard, Durden already cut over his left eye, and Costa just marched through positions: guard to side control, side control to the back, body triangle sunk in. Costa cranked on the neck and somehow Durden survived the first attempt, but Costa adjusted, got it under the chin, and submitted him at 2:19 of the round.


Durden got absolutely outclassed out there, and it is time for a real discussion about whether this guy is UFC caliber. Maybe it's an activity problem, since he fights way too much, but he loses the vast majority of his bouts. Pretty pathetic stuff when you remember Costa took this on two weeks' notice and outclassed him in the grappling, which is supposed to be Durden's strong suit. Good dominant win from Costa. Give him a ranked opponent next.
