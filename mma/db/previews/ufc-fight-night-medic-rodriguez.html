Overview

This is Serbia's first-ever UFC card, and the promotion is leaning hard on that novelty because the matchmaking itself doesn't have much star power to sell. Uroš Medić headlining is the kind of thing that works on paper (hometown finisher, undefeated stretch of first-round knockouts) but this is a fight that would look at home on a Tuesday Apex card, not a sold-out arena in Belgrade. It says something about the current state of the welterweight division that a three-fight prospect gets a five-round main event slot with almost no ranked names around him.

That said, the story behind the main event is genuinely wild. Daniel Rodriguez isn't just a "returning veteran," he's coming off an eight-month stint in a Mexican prison after a cannabis possession charge at the Tijuana border turned into a felony case, and he's headlining his first UFC main event less than four months after getting out. Whatever happens on Saturday, that's a real story.

The co-main is a study in opposite trajectories. Jan Błachowicz is 43, winless in four straight, and stepping in on short notice against Navajo Stirling, an undefeated prospect who by his own admission took the fight off a bender while on vacation. Stirling is favored, but a former champion getting live-betting underdog odds against an untested 10-0 talent on short notice is exactly the kind of gamble that makes for a swingy fight.

Beyond the top two, the card leans heavily on hometown storylines and total unknowns: Aleksandar Rakić making a desperate heavyweight jump after four straight losses, Duško Todorović and Vlasto Čepo and Miloš Janičić all fighting in front of home or regional crowds, and a prelim card stuffed with UFC debutants who nobody outside hardcore MMA circles has scouted. It's a card built for Serbian fans first and a betting/analysis audience second, but there are a handful of live stylistic questions worth digging into.


Main Card Picks

Uroš Medić vs. Daniel Rodriguez

Uroš Medić (13-3) enters as the hometown finisher riding a three-fight winning streak in which every win has come by first-round knockout, a 100 percent finishing rate that has him ranked inside the welterweight top 15. This is also his first scheduled five-round UFC fight, a real unknown given how quickly his fights have ended so far. Daniel Rodriguez (20-5) is the wily veteran on the other side, a fighter who has shared the cage with the likes of Kevin Lee, Ian Machado Garry, and Kelvin Gastelum, and who has nine knockouts, four submissions, and seven decision wins on his resume, proof he doesn't need one path to get it done. He also arrives with maybe the strangest backstory on the card: an eight-month stretch in a Mexican prison on a cannabis charge that nearly cost him a chunk of his prime, now thrust into a main event less than four months after his release. Betting markets have this lopsided, Medić a -400 favorite to Rodriguez's +300, and the stylistic read backs that up: a home-crowd knockout artist against a durable party-spoiler who hasn't fought in over a year.

Pick: Uroš Medić

Method: KO/TKO

Star power aside, this is a genuinely fun stylistic puzzle, and Medić is the pick. He's a kill-or-be-killed fighter whose fights consistently deliver, and he gets to do it in front of a Belgrade crowd that will be fully behind him from the walkout. Rodriguez, meanwhile, is coming off a year-long layoff, eight months of which he spent in a Mexican prison, and it's hard to see how he overcomes that kind of inactivity against a finisher this hot. At 39, Rodriguez has a track record of coming up short whenever he's tried to compete with upper-echelon welterweights, and even against fighters a tier below that. Expect the arena to be firmly in Medić's corner, expect him to come out swinging early, and expect him to put Rodriguez away in the first round.

Jan Błachowicz vs. Navajo Stirling

Jan Błachowicz (29-11-2) is 43 years old and winless in his last four fights (0-2-2), without a win since he defended the light heavyweight title against Israel Adesanya at UFC 259. He was originally booked for a rematch with Bogdan Guskov, but when Guskov pulled out to take the UFC Abu Dhabi main event against Magomed Ankalaev on short notice, Navajo Stirling stepped into the co-main slot instead. Stirling (10-0) is unbeaten across a five-fight UFC stint with two stoppage wins already banked in 2026, and by his own admission he took this fight on short notice while coming off "a massive bender" on vacation. Betting markets have inverted the pedigree gap here: Stirling is favored at -315 to -325, with Błachowicz sitting at +256 to +260. Błachowicz remains durable and technically sound but continues to battle the gas-tank issues that have defined his late-career slide, meaning his clearest path likely runs through an early finish before his conditioning becomes a factor.

Pick: Jan Błachowicz

Method: Decision

Błachowicz is the pick here. Simply put, Stirling isn't that good. He looks awkward on the feet, doesn't have incredible grappling, and hasn't fought anyone of real quality to this point in his career. There's no evidence he carries legitimate knockout power, and this represents a real step up in competition taken on short notice. The undefeated record has more to do with the light heavyweight division being a travesty at the moment than it does with Stirling's own level. Błachowicz may be 43, but he's still got some tread on the tires, and his gameplan has never revolved around speed to begin with. Expect him to lean on calf kicks early, gradually break Stirling down, and grind out a close 29-28 decision.

Aleksandar Rakić vs. Marcin Tybura

Aleksandar Rakić (14-6) makes his heavyweight debut in Belgrade riding the longest losing streak of his career at four straight, framing the weight jump as something his body needed "to recover" after years of cutting down to light heavyweight. He'll have the home crowd, but he's also aware that another loss pushes him to five straight without a win and drops him below .500 in the promotion. Marcin Tybura (27-11) is ranked No. 12 in the heavyweight division and is putting that ranking on the line against a natural light heavyweight moving up in size. Tybura himself has been streaky over his last five fights (2-3), most recently dropping back-to-back losses after a two-fight win streak. Algorithmic models favor Tybura at roughly 66 percent as the more established heavyweight, and the physical gap between a full-time heavyweight and a debutant moving up is the central question of this fight, one that a hometown crowd alone can't answer for Rakić.

Pick: Marcin Tybura

Method: Submission

The pick is Tybura. Rakić looks over the hill and has accumulated serious damage across his last several outings. On paper, he probably still has the skillset to beat Tybura, but at this point the mental side of the equation isn't there anymore after four straight losses. Expect Tybura to get this fight to the ground and find a submission in the first or second round.

Duško Todorović vs. Robert Valentin

Duško Todorović (13-6) makes his 11th UFC appearance on the biggest stage his home country has ever hosted. He's a meat-and-potatoes wrestle-boxer with a taekwondo base and holds a significant edge in output over Valentin, landing 4.7 significant strikes per minute compared to Valentin's 1.1. He's healthy, motivated, and fighting in front of a sold-out home crowd. Robert Valentin (12-6) is a Swiss finisher known for lunging offense and sneaky submission skills who just picked up his first UFC win, overwhelming his opponent for a quick tapout after a nightmare start to his Octagon career (he was stopped by eventual TUF winner Ryan Loder in the finale). Despite the hostile road environment, Valentin enters as the betting favorite at -180 to Todorović's +145, an interesting split given that both the crowd and the output numbers appear to favor the home fighter. Both men are coming off submission wins, and Todorović's volume and distance management give him tools to counter Valentin's wilder, lunging style.

Pick: Robert Valentin

Method: Decision

The pick is Valentin. His losses aren't as bad as the record suggests. He started his career on a three-fight skid, but two of those three losses came against Ateba Gautier and Torrez Finney, and neither loss looks that damning on closer inspection, particularly the Finney fight, where Valentin actually won one judge's scorecard because he arguably dealt more damage while Finney spent the fight just holding him down. Todorović, on the other hand, has been genuinely poor, with losses to nearly everyone he's faced in the middleweight division, including Zachary Reese. Valentin shouldn't lose to a Reese-level fighter, so by simple MMA math, he should be able to squeak out the win here.

Vlasto Čepo vs. Gilbert Urbina

Vlasto Čepo (14-3) makes his UFC debut on home soil in Belgrade after transitioning from boxing to MMA seven years ago. He enters off back-to-back knockout wins in Oktagon MMA, and nine of his 14 career wins have come by first-round knockout. Gilbert Urbina (7-4) is 1-3 inside the UFC since appearing on The Ultimate Fighter 29 and has dropped back-to-back knockout losses, including one to Uroš Medić, also on this card. He's hunting his first win since 2023, now on the road against a heavy-handed hometown debutant. Čepo is a significant betting favorite at -350 as the powerful, volume-striking newcomer, and most predictions lean toward a Čepo stoppage.

Pick: Vlasto Čepo

Method: KO/TKO

The pick is Čepo. It's his debut, so how good he actually is remains an open question, but Urbina is a known quantity at this point, and it isn't a flattering one. This has the feel of a matchup the UFC built to give Čepo a soft landing spot in his first fight, setting him up for a highlight-reel knockout in front of his home fans.

Miloš Janičić vs. Noah Gugnon

Miloš Janičić (19-3) becomes the first fighter from Montenegro to compete in the UFC, riding an eight-fight winning streak dating back to 2020 with zero trips to the judges' scorecards, his last five wins have all come by knockout. He's considered one of the more promising under-30 lightweights in Europe, a rangy striker with good combination punching and underused kicks. Noah Gugnon (9-2) is a 25-year-old Frenchman debuting on a five-fight winning streak, four of them by KO/TKO. He's a tall, long-limbed muay thai-influenced southpaw who works from range with strong close-quarters tools, and his stylistic profile shares broad similarities with Janičić's. This one opens the main card as a genuine pick 'em between two lightweight newcomers, one of the few truly close betting lines on the entire card given how similarly both fighters are built stylistically.

Pick: Miloš Janičić

Method: KO/TKO

This is a genuine pick 'em, but the nod goes to Janičić. He brings more experience into the fight, and while it's not technically a hometown crowd, his Montenegrin roots should still give him a regional-favorite atmosphere in Belgrade that Gugnon won't have.

Prelims Picks

Ľudovít Klein vs. Tofiq Musayev

Ľudovít Klein (24-5-1) carries real momentum into Belgrade, having won five of his last six. His most recent win over Mateusz Rębecki, also on this card, earned a Fight of the Night bonus but came with a broken ankle suffered in the third round, an injury that has kept him out of action since. Tofiq Musayev (23-6) carries heavy knockout power, with 18 of his 23 career wins coming by KO/TKO, and he bounced back from a first-round kimura loss with a Fight of the Night unanimous decision over Ignacio Bahamondes in March. Klein is favored at -230 to +190 on the strength of superior UFC experience and more well-rounded finishing skills, but Musayev's power and wrestling-oriented pressure present a genuine durability test for a Klein coming off a long injury layoff.

Pick: Ľudovít Klein

Method: Decision

The pick is Klein. Both men have wins over Bahamondes, but that doesn't mean much on its own given that Bahamondes fights like an absolute moron in the cage. Musayev's win over him was also outright controversial, marked by an illegal head clash and cage grabs, and he's fortunate he didn't lose a point for it. Klein has fought the better overall opposition and is coming off a grappling-heavy loss to Mateusz Gamrot that should have taught him plenty. Musayev, by contrast, hasn't fought anyone on Gamrot's level and was submitted by Myktybek Orolbai in his own UFC debut. Expect Klein to blend his skill set together more effectively and come away with a clean decision, somewhere in the range of 29-28 or 30-27.

Oban Elliott vs. Michael Oliveira

Oban Elliott (12-4), "The Welsh Gangster," enters on a two-fight skid after winning his first three UFC bouts and is looking to snap the streak against an unbeaten debutant. Michael Oliveira (9-0), nicknamed "PQD," makes his full Octagon debut off a Dana White's Contender Series knockout win in October 2025, with eight of his nine career wins coming by stoppage. He holds a sizable striking-output edge over Elliott, landing 8.40 significant strikes per minute to Elliott's 3.62, and carries a much heavier betting favorite tag at -357 to Elliott's +287. Oliveira's superior output and finishing rate make him the clear betting favorite here, and most analysts lean toward an Oliveira decision or stoppage given the gap in volume.

Pick: Oban Elliott

Method: Decision

This is a do-or-die fight for Elliott, a loss here almost certainly ends his UFC run, and he's the pick anyway. There isn't much known about Oliveira heading into his debut, and he has the profile of a can-crusher who's been fed soft competition on the way up. That said, it's still a tough matchup for a debut, since Elliott's only real issues over his last two fights have come on the ground, and he's moving up in weight into a matchup where he should actually be the more technical fighter on the feet. Oliveira's best chance is likely to catch Elliott early. Expect Elliott to fight strategically out of the gate, absorb whatever early onslaught comes his way, and find his rhythm in the second and third rounds to pull out a close 29-28 decision.

Borislav Nikolić vs. Mark Vologdin

Borislav Nikolić (16-2) stepped in on late notice after Josias Musasa pulled out of the original matchup with Vologdin. The 33-year-old makes his UFC debut riding a six-fight winning streak that includes a Brave CF title win and a successful defense, with finishes in all but one of those wins. He's a big bantamweight with good reach and a technical kickboxing base, fighting in his hometown of Belgrade. Mark Vologdin (12-4-2) makes his second UFC appearance following a majority draw against John Castaneda in his debut. He fights at a breakneck pace matched by few in the division outside of Merab Dvalishvili, competing with reckless abandon, the kind of style that produces either spectacular wins or spectacular losses. This is being billed as one of the most evenly matched bouts on the card, with Nikolić a modest home favorite at -180 and Vologdin at +150, needing to tune out a hostile Belgrade crowd for his first UFC win.

Pick: Mark Vologdin

Method: Decision

The pick is Vologdin. He isn't a great fighter, but there's no denying he has the grit to go the full three rounds. Nikolić, by contrast, still looks too green for this level and is likely to get shown some levels in his promotional debut.

Dennis Buzukja vs. Bogdan Grad

Dennis Buzukja (12-6), a Dana White's Contender Series alum who has gone 1-4 in five UFC appearances since, enters on a two-fight skid, most recently knocked out in the first round by Marcio Barbosa in April after a split-decision loss to Francis Marshall. Bogdan Grad (15-4), also a Contender Series alum and nicknamed "The Unleashed," is on his own two-fight skid, knocked out in 30 seconds by Luke Riley in November 2025 following a unanimous-decision loss to Muhammad Naimov. Both featherweights are trying to snap losing streaks here, and Grad's superior power and finishing instincts make him the modest betting favorite at -200 to Buzukja's +174 in a fight between two similarly slumping opponents.

Pick: Dennis Buzukja

Method: Decision

The pick is Buzukja. He's fought considerably better competition than Grad since arriving in the UFC and brings a more well-rounded approach, while Grad leans much more heavily on his grappling. Expect Grad to look for the takedown early, but he shouldn't find much success getting Buzukja down. Ultimately, Buzukja should be able to put the whole package together better and squeak out a 29-28 decision.

Mateusz Rębecki vs. Kyle Prepolec

Mateusz Rębecki (20-5) fights on home turf after three straight Fight of the Night–caliber wars gave way to a three-fight losing streak, including a loss to Ľudovít Klein, also on this card. He's a southpaw who lands 5.21 significant strikes per minute at 46 percent accuracy and averages 3.38 takedown attempts per 15 minutes, a genuine two-way threat on paper. Kyle Prepolec ("Killshot," 18-10) is also trying to snap a two-fight skid, having gone to war with Drew Dober last October for a bonus. His pure striking style is a clear contrast to Rębecki's grappling-heavy approach. Neither man has won in his last outing, adding some unpredictability, but Rębecki is a heavy home favorite at -550 to +400, and UFC media has flagged this as a potential Fight of the Night frontrunner given both fighters' action tendencies.

Pick: Mateusz Rębecki

Method: Decision

The pick is Rębecki. Prepolec is a gatekeeper at this point in his career, nothing more. Rębecki isn't world-class either, but he should be good enough to get the job done and pick up a decision win at home.

Nina Nikolija Milošević vs. Hailey Cowan

Nina Nikolija Milošević (8-1), born in Sweden but representing Serbia and nicknamed "Queen Beast," lost her pro debut before rattling off eight straight wins, five by finish, four of those in the first round. She's a promising hometown prospect making her UFC debut. Hailey Cowan (7-5), a Dana White's Contender Series graduate still chasing her first UFC win, is on a three-fight skid, most recently losing to Alice Pereira in April. She trains at Xtreme Couture and holds a height and reach advantage, with far more experience against UFC-level competition than anyone Milošević has faced to date. Cowan's experience against tougher opposition is her biggest asset against a hometown prospect making her debut, and she'll be looking for Milošević to make a rookie mistake.

Pick: Nina Nikolija Milošević

Method: KO/TKO

Honestly, no strong read on this one. Milošević is making her debut, and Cowan has been getting beaten up by UFC-level competition since she arrived in the promotion. It's also hard to imagine the UFC building this kind of hometown-debut spotlight if they weren't fairly confident in the outcome. Milošević gets the nod here, pulling off a TKO win via ground and pound in the first or second round.

Jovan Leka vs. Alexander Poppeck

Jovan Leka (13-2) makes his UFC debut on a six-fight winning streak, four by stoppage, having won a Contender Series bout in October 2025 before notching an arm-triangle submission and a TKO in his last two fights. He stands 6-foot-5 with a 79-inch reach. Alexander Poppeck (21-5) steps in on 10 days' notice replacing Max Gimenis, also debuting himself, riding an unbeaten run dating back to 2023 with finishes in three of his last four fights. He typically competes at light heavyweight and is giving up size for this heavyweight debut. Two hungry debutants in an exciting stylistic matchup, Leka is favored at -220 to -230 on home turf and finishing pedigree, though Poppeck's short-notice step-up adds a real layer of unpredictability.

Pick: Jovan Leka

Method: KO/TKO

Two debutants here that are hard to know much about, but the pick is Leka. He's been making relatively easy work of the competition he's faced on the regional scene, and paired with his hometown status in Belgrade, that's enough of an edge to lean his way.

Marina Spasić vs. Stephanie Luciano

Marina Spasić ("Maki Beki," 7-1), a 32-year-old Belgrade native and former Balkan K-1 kickboxer, becomes the first Serbian woman to fight in the UFC, making her promotional debut in her hometown off an April finish of Samara Santos at ARMMADA 17. Six of her seven career wins have come by stoppage. Stephanie Luciano (7-2-1), a Brazilian Contender Series alum from the Class of 2023, has gone 2-1 in the UFC and most recently submitted Ravena Oliveira last October in Vancouver. She's the younger, more explosive athlete with a more complete skill set on paper. Spasić's grappling remains a real question mark against quality opposition, and if Luciano forces resets with lateral movement while threatening takedowns, she has several paths to a decision win despite fighting on the road against a hometown debutant.

Pick: Stephanie Luciano

Method: Decision

The pick is Luciano. This is a difficult matchup for anyone's UFC debut. Luciano has already fought legitimate competition in Talita Alencar and Sam Hughes since arriving in the promotion, and that gap in experience is too much to expect Spasić to close in her first fight. This looks like too big a jump in competition, and Spasić should struggle with it.
