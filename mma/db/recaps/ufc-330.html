Summary

UFC 330 was, to put it plainly, a shit fucking event.


Islam Makhachev delivered his second stinker in a row as the promotion's biggest star, and that's a real problem when he was the entire selling point of the card. Makhachev spent five rounds either smothering Ian Machado Garry against the fence or holding him there and waiting out the clock, and by the championship rounds he had nothing left offensively beyond leaning on Garry at the cage. It was boring as hell, and it left nobody excited about Makhachev as the new 170-pound king. He simply can't dominate the welterweights the way he ran through lightweight, and his fights are going to keep looking like this until something changes. Garry, for his part, did enough in the second half of the fight to make it competitive, peppering Makhachev with oblique kicks and catching him clean against the fence, but Dana White was reportedly unimpressed with his showing, and Garry will need a handful of wins to get back into the title picture.


The other title fight was arguably worse. Mackenzie Dern spent five rounds taking Gillian Robertson down again and again, threatening rear-naked chokes and an armbar that she couldn't finish, while Robertson had nothing to offer once her own takedowns stopped working. It was a genuinely terrible fight, the kind that serves as a reminder of why the promotion doesn't expand women's MMA further or run more women's title fights. Neither woman did anything worth caring about going forward.


Thank god for Jalin Turner, who blitzed Kauê Fernandes in under a minute and picked up a performance bonus, because there honestly wasn't much else memorable about this show.


Final Score: 5.9/10

Main Card

Islam Makhachev(c) vs. Ian Machado Garry

Result: Decision (unanimous) (49–46, 49–46, 48–47) at R5 5:00

Islam Makhachev overwhelmed Ian Machado Garry with grappling in round one, and the Irishman had little response to offer, a slow start in his first defense at 170. Round two was more of the same, Makhachev controlling Garry against the fence and on the ground, though a lot of it amounted to stalling. On a clinch break, Islam's toes clipped Garry's chin on a head kick and briefly put him down. From there the fight flipped. Garry started using the fence far more effectively to deny Makhachev's grappling in round three, catching fire at range and peppering him with oblique kicks. He kept it up in round four, denying every grappling attempt, and Makhachev's only real offense at that point was simply holding Garry against the fence, something Garry used to his advantage by landing shots on Islam while pinned there. By round five, Garry was tagging Makhachev regularly, and out of desperation, Islam got the fight to the ground and held Garry in a body lock to run out the clock.


Boring as hell. Nobody's thrilled that Islam won given how he won it, and now the sport has to deal with Islam as the welterweight king. He simply can't dominate at 170 the way he dominated at lightweight, and his performances have become genuinely hard to watch as a result. Dana White reportedly wasn't happy with how Garry looked either; he'll need to string together a handful of wins to get back into the title picture.

Mackenzie Dern(c) vs. Gillian Robertson

Result: Decision (unanimous) (49–46, 49–46, 48–47) at R5 5:00

Mackenzie Dern was the more aggressive fighter from the start, and once an early takedown attempt from Gillian Robertson was stuffed against the cage, Dern took over completely. She scored a takedown of her own, worked from half guard into the back, and hunted a rear-naked choke that Robertson somehow survived to end round one. Dern kept the pattern going for the next four rounds, repeatedly taking Robertson down, threatening chokes and an armbar that she couldn't quite finish, and even outboxing her on the feet in round three while Robertson did essentially nothing standing. Robertson had a couple of moments late, landing some upper cuts in the clinch and getting on top with soft ground strikes in round five, but it was never enough to change the shape of the fight.


Terrible fight. Robertson really had nothing once her takedowns stopped working; it was a pathetic showing that looked like she didn't belong in the UFC out there. This fight is a reminder of why the promotion doesn't expand women's MMA further and doesn't run more women's title fights. What a terrible fight. Hard to care about what's next for either of them after that.

Jalin Turner vs. Kauê Fernandes

Result: KO (punches) at R1 0:39

Kauê Fernandes opened with a body kick, but it barely mattered. Jalin Turner answered with a left hand that put Fernandes down, climbed on top, and rained down punishment until the referee stepped in, needing less than a minute to get the job done.


No idea what happened with the fight odds on that one. Turner made easy work of it, completely running through the Brazilian in the first round. He could be making a real run at the top 10 in the lightweight division now, and he's already calling out a ranked opponent next. He probably deserves it.

Dustin Stoltzfus vs. Mansur Abdul-Malik

Result: Submission (rear-naked choke) at R2 4:25

The two traded in the center to open the fight before Dustin Stoltzfus scored a takedown and took the back of Mansur Abdul-Malik. Stoltzfus complained to referee Keith Peterson about an eye poke off a Malik head kick attempt but was told to keep fighting, and he answered by picking Malik up and slamming him in the center of the Octagon, advancing to full mount and unloading heavy ground and pound before taking the back again and nearly locking up a suloev stretch as the round ended.


Malik came out swinging in round two and briefly turned the fight into a genuine firefight, even landing some ground and pound of his own out of half guard after a scramble reversed the position. Stoltzfus started showing damage on his face, but he kept coming, using the fence to deny a Malik takedown attempt before locking Malik up in a guillotine against the cage. He abandoned that choke, continued to lean on Malik, and after some more ground and pound and a scramble, finally sank in a rear-naked choke to finish it.


Abdul-Malik really looked terrible out there. He honestly doesn't look like he's UFC caliber at this point; he couldn't get anything going on the feet or on the ground. Stoltzfus looked reasonable in the win, but it's hard to know how much weight to put on it. Did Stoltzfus actually look good, or did Abdul-Malik just look that bad?

Esteban Ribovics vs. Edson Barboza

Result: TKO (punches) at R2 1:32

Esteban Ribovics came out on the gas, moving forward and sniping Edson Barboza from range. Barboza was cut over the eye almost immediately, and his lead shin was completely chewed up by the end of the round. He got dropped right before the bell but survived to hear it. Ribovics stayed aggressive to start round two, though he was clearly pulling off the gas by that point, and he still managed to badly hurt Barboza, who started running away and got pieced up against the cage until the fight was stopped.


This one probably should have been stopped in between rounds one and two. Barboza was getting his ass beat out there and wasn't really fighting back. It's time for him to retire; that was hard to watch. It's also not clear what the point of giving Ribovics that matchup was in the first place, it looked like he was beating up on a senior citizen.

Prelims

Chidi Njokuani vs. Joel Álvarez

Result: Decision (unanimous) (30–27, 30–27, 29–28) at R3 5:00

Chidi Njokuani looked sharp early, working calf kicks and teeps to the body, while Joel Álvarez walked him down and put together combinations of his own, including a couple of low kicks. Njokuani answered with a head kick to close out the round. From there, though, the fight turned into a snoozer. Round two saw both guys point fighting with little action, briefly interrupted by an eye poke from Njokuani, and round three was more of the same, with the two clinching against the cage and doing next to nothing.


Álvarez looked terrible out there. He did essentially nothing throughout the entire fight and still ended up losing the decision, which honestly serves him right. What was the game plan supposed to be? Njokuani did just enough to get the decision, but not enough to gain any new fans off this one.

Charles Johnson vs. Eduardo Henrique

Result: Submission (twister) at R3 1:36

Eduardo Chapolin sprawled and used a head lock to stop an early takedown from Charles Johnson, and the two spent the opening stretch of round one feeling each other out, with Chapolin landing a right hand on a break and later hurting Johnson with a right hook that may or may not have been an eye poke. Chapolin got the fight to the ground, but as Johnson postured up, he ate an illegal knee to a downed opponent, and the action stopped. The replay confirmed the original sequence was in fact an eye poke, and referee Herzog deducted a point from Chapolin.


Chapolin took over in round two, dropping Johnson with a big left hook and stalking him around the Octagon, piecing him up as Johnson resorted to desperation takedowns and eventually pulled guard just to survive. Johnson recovered but wasn't doing much by the end of the round. He came out much more aggressive in round three, landing an elbow up close and hurting Chapolin with a body teep. When Chapolin shot a desperation takedown, Johnson sprawled and worked to take the back, baiting Chapolin into a worse position before locking up a Scottish twister for the finish.


The point deduction had to happen there, and good on Herzog for taking it. This was a crazy win for Johnson: he was fouled, hurt, and losing the fight on the cards, and still found a way to get the finish. Tough break for Chapolin, who had him dead to rights in round two. Johnson continues to find ways to prolong his career, and the uncs are cleaning up on this card.

Donte Johnson vs. Eric McConico Jr.

Result: KO (punches) at R1 1:38

Donte Johnson came out aggressive from the opening bell, hurting Eric McConico early, though McConico briefly looked for a takedown that never materialized and even stung Johnson back with a jab. Johnson stayed reckless throughout, walking straight into McConico's shots, but it didn't matter. He landed a shot that sent McConico into a different dimension and followed up with ground and pound to finish it.


That last shot was completely unnecessary. Johnson should be fined or something for that, the referee was already pulling him off the guy and he kept trying to land more. He knows the rules, and the adrenaline excuse doesn't fly here. That said, this result was expected. McConico was a sacrificial lamb out there.

Tresean Gore vs. Vicente Luque

Result: Decision (unanimous) (30–27, 30–27, 29–28) at R3 5:00

Tresean Gore and Vicente Luque traded jabs early and tagged each other, with Gore working in calf kicks along the way. Gore did enough over the rest of the fight to get his hand raised on the scorecards.

Early Prelims

Lucas Fernando vs. Rafael Tobias

Result: TKO (knee to the body and elbows) at R3 1:10

The two traded heavy shots early, with both Lucas Fernando and Rafael Tobias hurting each other badly in round one. Fernando took over in round two, dropping Tobias with a jab before letting him back up and continuing to stalk him down, tagging him almost at will with a right hand and a head kick along the way. The two kept swinging in round three until Fernando landed a knee to the body that completely crippled Tobias, who crumbled to the canvas. Fernando finished him off with ground and pound shortly after.


Great debut from Fernando. Unfortunately, Tobias just isn't that good. Fernando, though, is a big guy with a big frame who looks like an absolute killer, and this looks like a potentially great signing for the UFC.

Neil Magny vs. Ramiz Brahimaj

Result: TKO (punches) at R2 3:20

Ramiz Brahimaj took over from the opening bell, taking Neil Magny down and smothering him with heavy ground pressure to win the first round clearly. He got another early takedown in round two, but the pace clearly started to catch up with him. Once Brahimaj wore himself out, Magny found the opening to reverse the position, and from there he turned the fight completely around, finishing Brahimaj with punches before the round was out.

Jeremiah Wells vs. Myktybek Orolbai

Result: Technical Submission (ninja choke) at R3 1:24

Jeremiah Wells came out swinging, stinging Myktybek Orolbai with punches and briefly clinching against the cage before Orolbai caught a teep kick and used it to put Wells on the canvas, wearing on him with ground and pound to close the round. Wells landed a nice combination in round two, but Orolbai kept finding takedowns, working from Wells' back and threatening a kimura before Wells scrambled free, only for Orolbai to get right back on top of him. In round three, Wells cracked Orolbai badly with a right hand. Orolbai immediately shot for a takedown out of survival instinct, but walked straight into a guillotine choke, and Wells put him to sleep.


Unreal win for Wells. He was dead to rights and about to lose a decision, and somehow got it done anyway. Orolbai, on the other hand, did not look good out there. He was getting worked on the feet for most of the fight and couldn't finish a single submission on the ground once he got it there. There's clearly too much hype on Orolbai, and that was a pathetic performance from him. Wells has already said that if he doesn't get a top 15 matchup next, he'll retire.
