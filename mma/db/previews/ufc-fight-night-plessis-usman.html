Overview

The UFC returns to Oklahoma City for the first time since 2017, setting up shop at the Paycom Center with a card that is thin on rankings but heavy on intrigue. The formula here is simple: one legitimately fascinating main event between two former champions, one classic prospect-vs-gatekeeper co-main, and then a parade of undefeated prospects the UFC is trying to sort into "real" and "regional highlight reel" piles. Prelims kick off at 5pm ET with the main card at 8pm ET on Paramount+.

The headliner is the story: Dricus du Plessis against Kamaru Usman at middleweight, five rounds. Du Plessis is coming off the Chimaev beatdown that cost him the belt and ended an 11-fight win streak, and with Sean Strickland back on top of the division and DDP holding two wins over him, the path back to a title shot is right there. Usman, meanwhile, is 39 years old, coming off a 13-month layoff, and making his first full-camp appearance at 185 in pursuit of a two-division legacy play. The faceoffs have already set off alarm bells: the size difference between these two is ridiculous, and Usman's recent body of work raises real questions about how much of the Nigerian Nightmare is actually left.

The co-main is a referendum on Jared Cannonier. The 42-year-old former title challenger is 2-3 in his last five and drew Christian Leroy Duncan, a rangy British striker on a four-fight win streak with back-to-back spinning-technique knockouts. Duncan is around a -330 favorite, which tells you exactly how the market views a 42-year-old coming off a loss to Michael Page against the exact stylistic profile that has been beating him.

The rest of the card is prospect roulette. Chase Hooper gets the softest landing imaginable after two straight knockout losses. Fatima Kline, the hyped No. 11 strawweight, is favored over No. 8 Tabatha Ricci in a fight that will show whether the hype is warranted. Tommy McMillen and Alberto Montes meet in an unbeaten-vs-unbeaten featherweight showcase, and the prelims are stacked with undefeated newcomers, including an Elliott vs. Anderson bout that was thrown together on four days' notice between two men who have finished every opponent they have ever faced.


Main Card Picks

Dricus du Plessis vs. Kamaru Usman

Dricus du Plessis (23-3) makes his first walk since losing the middleweight title, and it was not a competitive loss: Khamzat Chimaev put a one-sided beatdown on him at UFC 319 that ended an 11-fight win streak and his seven-year unbeaten run. Before that, the resume was elite: two wins over current champion Sean Strickland, a face crank of Israel Adesanya, and a TKO of Robert Whittaker. Ranked No. 2, he intends to spoil this comeback tour and keep his top-5 spot, and he has been vocal about disliking the Strickland vs. Chimaev rematch talk while positioning himself for another crack at the belt.

Kamaru Usman (21-4) returns from a 13-month layoff for his first full-camp middleweight fight at 39 years old. His only previous appearance at 185 was the short-notice Chimaev fight at UFC 294, where he pushed Khamzat to a majority decision. His stated plan for 2026 is a two-division legacy run: he expected the Makhachev welterweight title shot after beating Joaquin Buckley, got passed over, and pivoted up a division instead, floating a Strickland rematch as "in play" with a win here. The winner has a legitimate claim to a middleweight title shot, and the faceoff already told part of the story: the size difference is ridiculous.

Pick: Dricus Du Plessis

Method: Decision

Usman is somewhat over the hill. Not so far gone that he has no shot, but close to it. The age and inactivity are concerning on their own, and something about the situation suggests he has been threatening retirement behind the scenes if he isn't handed specific matchups. That is not a good look: if you are threatening retirement, maybe it actually makes sense to retire. Look at his recent opponents and how he fared against them, and the pattern is clear: he has not seriously dominated anyone in the grappling lately unless they flat out cannot grapple, which is exactly what happened against Buckley. That means this is primarily a striking fight with some work done against the fence.

Dricus du Plessis has not fought in a year and looked like a complete chump against Khamzat in his last outing, but that performance was somewhat of a fluke: he either was not prepared for the grappling onslaught or failed to wear Khamzat out early. He will have learned from that loss, and with Strickland back as champion, there is enormous motivation to get back into the title picture. DDP will be too much for Kamaru Usman on the feet, Usman will not be able to outgrapple him, and this ends in a 49-46 decision with du Plessis clearly taking the win.

Jared Cannonier vs. Christian Leroy Duncan

Jared Cannonier (18-9) is a 42-year-old former title challenger and rankings mainstay who is now 2-3 in his last five, coming off a decision loss to Michael "Venom" Page. He has been on the roster for twelve years and ranked since his middleweight debut in 2018, and he is framing this one as "my redemption against the UK" after the Page defeat. The problem: he is clearly on the back nine of his career, and his recent losses have come against exactly the kind of rangy, athletic movers he is about to face again.

Christian Leroy Duncan (14-2, 7-2 UFC) is the ascending British striker on a four-fight win streak, with back-to-back highlight-reel spinning-technique knockouts of Eryk Anders and Marco Tulio before outpointing Roman Dolidze in March. This is his step-up fight: beat a name like Cannonier and he pushes into the upper tier of the rankings. The books have him around -330, roughly 75 percent implied, which says everything about how the market views a 42-year-old at this stage.

Pick: Christian Leroy Duncan

Method: Decision

Jared Cannonier is at a point in his career where his age is a serious concern. It is hard to pick a 42-year-old in good faith when he has been inactive and does not have any particularly strong skillset to hang his hat on. Cannonier will not be able to outgrapple Christian Leroy Duncan, so expect him to look to clinch with the Brit up against the cage. The fear is that Cannonier will not be active enough in the clinch, and CLD will get separation and go to work on the feet. He will tag Cannonier at will and get the job done with a 29-28 decision.

Chase Hooper vs. Mitch Ramirez

Chase Hooper (16-5-1) has hit a wall. After a five-fight win streak at lightweight capped by wins over Jim Miller, Clay Guida, and Viacheslav Borshchev, the 26-year-old submission specialist has been stopped by strikes in back-to-back fights against the more athletic Alexander Hernandez and Lance Gibson Jr. He has 12 finishes in 16 wins and is still young, but this is a must-win to stop the slide.

Mitch Ramirez (8-3, 0-2 UFC) is a 33-year-old with an 87.5 percent career finish rate who hits hard but has dropped both of his octagon appearances and is still hunting his first promotional win. Analysts point out he lacks the strength and athleticism of Hernandez and Gibson, the two men who beat Hooper, which is exactly why he opened around +285 in a fight where both men desperately need a result.

Pick: Chase Hooper

Method: Decision

Chase Hooper kind of ducks, but this is as clear a layup as they can give the PNW native without just handing him a win on his record. Hooper is coming off two brutal KO losses to Hernandez and Gibson, but Mitch Ramirez is straight fucking dogshit. Hooper will go out there, get Ramirez to the ground, and hold him there for three rounds on his way to a 30-27 decision. Expect a snoozefest: Hooper's submission skills and striking ability are effectively non-existent, so this is three rounds of top control and nothing else.

Tabatha Ricci vs. Fatima Kline

Tabatha Ricci (12-4, 7-4 UFC) is the No. 8-ranked strawweight, a 5'1" Brazilian judo and grappling-based fighter coming off getting outworked by recent title challenger Virna Jandiroba. She volunteered for this one on short notice after Amanda Ribas withdrew, says the short camp was "perfect," and claims she is "next in line" with a win. Notably, she has never been finished in the UFC.

Fatima Kline (9-1) is the No. 11-ranked surging prospect on a three-fight run with three finishes in her last four, her only career loss coming up a division against Jasmine Jasudavicius. She holds a five-inch height advantage, opened as a sizable favorite around -255 despite the ranking gap, and the UFC is actively promoting her as one of this card's "Fighters on the Rise." A top-10 spot is on the line.

Pick: Tabatha Ricci

Method: Decision

There is a lot of hype on Fatima Kline coming into this matchup, and this fight will be an indication of whether it is warranted or not. There are real reservations and concerns about the tri-state area native, given that she has only crushed cans so far in her UFC career. A win over Angela Hill does not really mean or show much about the caliber of fighter she is. This is going to be a wake up call for Kline: her only real challenge to date was the loss to Jasmine Jasudavicius. Tabatha Ricci is going to be too much for the prospect, and Kline ends up losing a decision.

Tommy McMillen vs. Alberto Montes

Tommy McMillen (10-0) is the undefeated Montana featherweight out of Red Hawk Academy, where he trains with Sean O'Malley and Tim Welch, and "Gun" is being hyped as a potential featherweight version of Suga Sean. Nearly all of his wins are finishes, including a first-round bonus-winning stoppage of Manolo Zecchini in his April debut. He says this was "the healthiest fight camp of my life" and that he grew more in this camp than in his entire career combined.

Alberto Montes (10-1) looked slick in his own debut, taking out TUF 29 winner Ricky Turcios with a second-round technical submission in March, and the UFC lists "The Promise" among the card's "Fighters on the Rise." Two unbeaten-in-the-UFC featherweights, and the winner gets fast-tracked in a talent-rich 145 division. This is exactly the kind of fight the UFC books to find out which prospect is real.

Pick: Tommy McMillen

Method: KO/TKO

Tommy McMillen is not that good and will eventually get fraud checked by a legitimate contender, but that is not happening this weekend. McMillen has a background in grappling with a handful of submission victories to his name, and he has legitimate striking ability that will let him hang with most of the division on the feet. His real flaw is that he gets hit a lot, and that will be his inevitable downfall down the line. In this one, though, Alberto Montes will not be able to implement his grappling approach and will be forced to strike with McMillen on the feet. Eventually he falls and loses the bout.

Prelims Picks

Austin Bashi vs. Jose Miguel Delgado

Austin Bashi (14-1, 1-1 UFC) is a burly wrestle-grappler who secures clean takedowns and thrives in scrambles, though his striking remains a work in progress. He rebounded from his first career loss to Christian Rodriguez with a first-round throttling of John Yannis last August, and media are calling him a prospect ready for takeoff if he gets past his Contender Series classmate.

Jose Miguel Delgado (11-2, 3-1 UFC) is the rangy kickboxer of the pair, with sharp striking and improving defensive wrestling. He bounced back from a competitive loss to Nathaniel Wood by beating the eternally tough Andre Fili in March. This is a near pick'em between DWCS Season 8 classmates for the unofficial title of the season's best featherweight product, and analysts note Delgado has the same tools Rodriguez used to stymie Bashi: calf kicks and the ability to turn takedowns into scrambles.

Pick: Austin Bashi

Method: Decision

Austin Bashi's grappling is going to be too much for Jose Miguel Delgado. Bashi will put on a furious pace early and not give Delgado an inch to work with. With that approach, it becomes inevitable: Bashi blankets the Mexican for three rounds and takes the victory.

Jean-Paul Lebosnoyani vs. Ko Seok-hyeon

Jean-Paul Lebosnoyani (10-2) is the 27-year-old "Mufasa," a 5'11" welterweight with a 72-inch reach who is unbeaten so far in the UFC, most recently taking a split decision over Phil Rowe. Despite the perfect start, he comes in around a +200 underdog.

Ko Seok-hyeon (13-2, 2-0 UFC) is "The Korean Tyson," a squat, powerful southpaw who walks opponents down, bullies them into the fence, and drags them to the floor. He is a judo black belt and former combat sambo world champion who earned his contract by outpointing undefeated Igor Cavalcanti as a heavy underdog on the Contender Series, then beat the same Phil Rowe by unanimous decision in November. Two perfect UFC starts collide, both men hold recent wins over the same opponent, and one unbeaten run ends in Oklahoma.

Pick: Ko Seok-hyeon

Method: Decision

This is a clash of styles: Ko Seok-hyeon has a serious edge in the grappling, while Jean-Paul Lebosnoyani has the edge in the striking. Ko's grappling is simply going to be too much for Lebosnoyani. He will have his way with him from the opening bell and cruise to a 30-27 decision.

Levi Rodrigues Jr. vs. Felipe Franco

Levi Rodrigues Jr. (6-0) is "Baby Monster," a 29-year-old Brazilian muay thai black belt with somewhere between 20 and 30 Thai boxing wins making his UFC debut. His resume is as simple as it gets: six professional fights, six first-round finishes, every single one by strikes (one of them, over Freddy Vidal, was overturned due to a positive test). He has already announced ambitions of chasing belts in three weight classes.

Felipe Franco (10-2, 0-1 UFC) returns to his natural light heavyweight division after dropping a competitive short-notice decision to Mario Pinto in London while fighting up at heavyweight. It's a Brazilian-vs-Brazilian scrap at 205 between two finishers, and the question is whether "Baby Monster" is a real prospect or just a regional highlight reel.

Pick: Levi Rodrigues Jr.

Method: KO/TKO

This one is tough to call because neither of them has really done anything in the UFC. The main data point available is that Felipe Franco looked like an absolute dud in his debut against Mario Pinto. There is not much to go off of with Levi Rodrigues Jr. either, but there is enough to believe he gets the job done: even though it was overturned to a no contest, his finish of Freddy Vidal on the Contender Series was vicious. Rodrigues gets the finish inside the distance.

Ezra Elliott vs. Damien Anderson

This one was thrown together on roughly four days' notice, and it might be the most fun fight on the prelims: two undefeated newcomers who have finished every opponent they have ever faced. Ezra Elliott (7-0) is a 26-year-old Arizona prospect out of The MMA Lab and Red Hawk Academy, one of Sean O'Malley's main training partners, with polish on the ground and knockout power. He is coming off a first-round knockout of Ashtin Caniglia in May.

Damien Anderson (5-0) is "Demon Hands," a 29-year-old Texan grappling ace who transitioned from competitive BJJ, trains with Nick Rodriguez, and brings modern leg-lock craft with four submissions in five finishes. The catch: he is a natural bantamweight taking this fight at featherweight on the same short notice. Elliott has the size, power, and camp pedigree; Anderson has the submission ceiling but is undersized.

Pick: Ezra Elliott

Method: Submission

This is a good matchup between two hot prospects. The edge goes to Ezra Elliott because he has looked strong on the regional scene and has been struggling to get a bump in competition for a while now. Elliott pulls off the submission win in the second round.

Alden Coria vs. Stewart Nicoll

Alden Coria (2-0 UFC, unbeaten in six) is the 28-year-old "Cobra," a longtime training partner of flyweight champion Joshua Van. He is a crisp, accurate combination puncher with underrated wrestling and a tall, rangy frame for the division, coming off a hometown win over Luis Gurule. He has said he wants an early finish on the feet and is eyeing the top 15, and the UFC is actively pushing him as one of the card's fighters to watch. Tapology community picks have him at 97 percent.

Stewart Nicoll (0-3 UFC) is the Australian "Kakamora," who arrived as an undefeated prospect with strong offensive wrestling that simply has not translated to this level. He has lost each of his three UFC starts in three different ways, including to Alessandro Costa earlier this year, and is widely framed as fighting for his job.

Pick: Alden Coria

Method: Decision

Neither of these guys is necessarily the truth in the flyweight division, but Stewart Nicoll is not UFC caliber. He has lost all three of his UFC fights, and it is not like the guys beating him are on some unbelievable win streak: these are fighters with losses on their own records too. Alden Coria points his way to a decision, and Nicoll ultimately gets cut from the promotion.

RJ Harris vs. Alvin Hines

RJ Harris (5-0) is a 27-year-old, 6'6", 260-pound undefeated newcomer stepping in for the injured Allen Frye Jr. to make his UFC debut, with four finishes in five wins, three of them submissions, most recently a second-round guillotine of Contender Series alum Phillip Latu. His game is built entirely around his size: a long lead hand to manage range and standing guillotine attempts whenever shorter, quicker opponents close the distance.

Alvin Hines (0-1 UFC) is the 34-year-old "Goozie," a compact, stocky 260-pounder who is more technical than most heavyweights, with basic boxing combinations that carry power, untelegraphed head kicks, and some wrestling. He dropped his debut decision to Jhonata Diniz. Fun wrinkle: Hines submitted the same Phillip Latu in his own pro debut back in 2023, giving this all-American heavyweight prelim a shared measuring stick.

Pick: RJ Harris

Method: Decision

It is genuinely hard to decide who wins this one. There was a good amount of hype surrounding Alvin Hines, but he looked terrible in his debut. He got absolutely smoked. On top of that, he is now coming off a PED suspension, and it was not a tainted supplement situation either. It is honestly hard to understand why the UFC is keeping him on the roster. Guys who come back from PED suspensions typically are not the same: they have lost a step. RJ Harris beats Hines for a decision win.

Anna Melisano vs. Dione Barbosa

Anna Melisano (6-1) makes her UFC debut on short notice, replacing the injured Veronica Hardy. "Bandana" is a TUF 34 contestant riding a lengthy win streak, known for aggressive muay thai striking and a relentless pace, with a big, long-limbed frame she puts to work behind frequent stance switches and long kicks and jabs from both sides. Her last two wins were finishes: a third-round doctor stoppage of Andrea Amaro and a first-round rear naked choke of Lydia Warren.

Dione Barbosa (9-4, 3-2 UFC) is the 34-year-old Brazilian "Witch" with a deep judo pedigree: seven-time Brazilian national champion, two-time Pan-American champion, and former national team member, with dangerous clinch grappling and submissions. She is coming off a majority decision over Melissa Gatto in April. The consensus framing is simple: Melisano needs to keep this fight long, because the first clinch with Barbosa likely means a trip and top pressure.

Pick: Anna Melisano

Method: Decision

It is not entirely clear what to make of this fight. Dione Barbosa does not inspire much confidence, but Anna Melisano is making her debut. On the regional scene, Melisano looks like she has elevated her game lately, and she has the skills to stop a fight on the feet or on the ground. Expect Melisano to struggle a bit in this one given the bump in competition, but she still ultimately pulls through and gets the job done.
