Overview

UFC 330 is a disgrace of a numbered card. Two title fights at the top do not make up for a bloated, top-heavy lineup that thins out into replacement fighters, catchweight scrambles, and UFC debutants who have no business anywhere near a pay-per-view. This is a card held together by champion star power and not much else.

The main event is Islam Makhachev defending the welterweight title he took off Jack Della Maddalena against Ian Machado Garry, who talked his way into this shot by outstriking Belal Muhammad. A win extends Makhachev's unbeaten run to 17 straight, breaking Anderson Silva's UFC record. This is Makhachev's first defense of the 170-pound strap after a run of dominant lightweight title defenses, and Garry gets a shot at history mostly because there was nobody more deserving standing in line.

The co-main is Mackenzie Dern making the first defense of the strawweight title she won off Virna Jandiroba, against Gillian Robertson, who owns the UFC women's records for most career submissions and finishes and arrives on a five-fight tear. Two live grapplers in a title fight, at least this one has some intrigue to it.

Past the two title fights, the card falls apart fast. Jalin Turner and Kauê Fernandes both arrive off first-round knockouts. Edson Barboza, 40 years old and on a three-fight skid, is probably fighting the last bout of his UFC career against Esteban Ribovics. Mansur Abdul-Malik is a -700 favorite over a fading Dustin Stoltzfus, hardly a competitive main card fight. And the prelims are a mess of late changes: Joel Álvarez stepping in on 11 days' notice, Eduardo Henrique making his UFC debut on four days' notice in a catchweight bout that only exists because the original matchup fell apart.

The undercard is filler stacked on filler: an unbeaten prospect nobody outside hardcore fans has heard of in Donte Johnson, two UFC debutants nobody's clamoring to see in Rafael Tobias vs. Lucas Fernando, aging gatekeepers in Neil Magny and Ramiz Brahimaj, and a hometown opener for Jeremiah Wells against Myktybek Orolbai, the largest favorite on the entire card at -1050. Two title fights carrying an otherwise forgettable night.


Main Card Picks

Islam Makhachev(c) vs. Ian Machado Garry

Islam Makhachev enters as the No. 1 pound-for-pound fighter in the sport, riding a 16-fight win streak and defending the welterweight title he took off Jack Della Maddalena in November. Before moving up, he defended lightweight gold repeatedly, running through Dustin Poirier and Alexander Volkanovski twice. A win here breaks Anderson Silva's 16-fight UFC unbeaten record.

Ian Machado Garry is 17-1, with his only loss coming by decision to Shavkat Rakhmonov. He punched his ticket to this title shot by outstriking Belal Muhammad, and has also beaten Carlos Prates and Michael Page along the way. He brings crisp, technical boxing and underrated takedown defense, but has never faced anything resembling Makhachev's chain wrestling and top control.

Pick: Islam Makhachev

Method: Decision

Garry as champion would be bad for the sport, plain and simple, and thankfully it isn't happening. The gap in grappling here is enormous. Makhachev's sambo background is fundamentally more offensive than the jiu jitsu Garry has trained; sambo is built to attack and finish, jiu jitsu is built to survive and counter. Garry has shown solid takedown defense against strikers, but he has never faced anyone who picks and chooses his shots the way Makhachev does, and the Belal Muhammad win doesn't prove he can stop it. Garry's grappling is better than Jack Della Maddalena's, so he probably survives on the ground rather than getting finished, but this turns into a lay-and-pray affair with Makhachev in top position for long stretches. Garry's only real path is a knockout, and he doesn't have the power or the forward pressure to find one, he spends too much of his fights on the back foot. He might steal a round or two off Makhachev, but this ends as a clear 48-47 decision for the champion.

Mackenzie Dern(c) vs. Gillian Robertson

Mackenzie Dern captured the vacant strawweight title with a unanimous decision over Virna Jandiroba last October and now makes her first defense. She's 16-5 with three straight wins, a submission ace whose ground game has carried her career.

Gillian Robertson holds the UFC women's record for most career submissions and finishes, and arrives on a five-fight streak that includes wins over Amanda Lemos and Marina Rodriguez, the resume that earned her this first career title shot. Early in her UFC run she struggled for consistency, but that turned around once she settled into strawweight in 2023.

Pick: Gillian Robertson

Method: Decision

Dern isn't good, and the Jandiroba decision that got her the belt wasn't a convincing win either. This is a matchup of two jiu jitsu specialists, but they operate completely differently: Robertson is an offensive grappler who forces the action and hunts for dominant position, while Dern is passive, more comfortable capitalizing on mistakes than creating them. Robertson's pressure is going to be a problem Dern doesn't have an answer for, and by the second half of the fight she's just absorbing pressure rather than doing anything with it. Dern survives the individual submission attempts, she's durable enough for that, but she loses clearly on volume and control over the distance.

Jalin Turner vs. Kauê Fernandes

Jalin Turner returned from a brief retirement to stop Edson Barboza in December, snapping a two-fight skid. Known for explosive early aggression, he either wins fights outright in the opening rounds or fades and leaves himself vulnerable as bouts wear on.

Kauê Fernandes has won three straight since dropping his promotional debut to Marc Diakiese in late 2023, sandwiching a decision over Guram Kutateladze between finishes of Mohammad Yahya and Harry Hardwick, the latter a first-round leg-kick stoppage that earned a Performance of the Night bonus. Both men arrive off first-round knockouts, and Fernandes' leg kicks have been a real weapon lately.

Pick: Jalin Turner

Method: KO/TKO

Turner looks like the best version of himself right now. The only real hesitation here is some suspicious line movement in the last 12 hours, but on pure form Turner is back and dangerous, ready to go out and finish Fernandes without much trouble.

Mansur Abdul-Malik vs. Dustin Stoltzfus

Mansur Abdul-Malik suffered his first pro loss, a third-round KO to Yousri Belgaroui in March, snapping momentum from a slick first-round guillotine finish of Antonio Trocoli the previous December. A hard-hitting middleweight with 7 knockouts among 9 wins and a strong collegiate wrestling background, he's back just five months later looking to reset.

Dustin Stoltzfus has dropped consecutive decisions to former title challenger Kelvin Gastelum and Nursulton Ruziboev, going 3-6 across his last nine UFC bouts. The Lancaster middleweight is fighting near a home crowd looking to snap the skid.

Pick: Mansur Abdul-Malik

Method: KO/TKO

Yousri Belgaroui is legitimately good, a fighter who should be nowhere near a "gatekeeper" label and belongs in the top 15 conversation, so the loss doesn't say much about Abdul-Malik. He's simply better than Stoltzfus across the board. This should be one-way traffic from the opening bell, and Abdul-Malik gets him out of there in the first or second round.

Edson Barboza vs. Esteban Ribovics

Edson Barboza, 40 years old and in his 15th year in the UFC, has lost three straight, and the door may be closing on his Octagon career. One of the greatest kickers in UFC history, the only man to finish opponents via low kick, body kick, and head kick, he's been knocked out in two of his last five losses.

Esteban Ribovics was wrestled into a submission loss last time out by Mateusz Gamrot, but had previously established himself as one of the division's top strikers, averaging 7.27 significant strikes landed per minute.

Pick: Esteban Ribovics

Method: Decision

It's time for Barboza to retire. He's put on some banger fights and has good wins to his name, but he's old now and this should be it. Ribovics isn't anything special either, but he'll be enough to get past Barboza, who's on a three-fight losing streak. This has to be the last fight of his career.

Prelims Picks

Chidi Njokuani vs. Joel Álvarez

Chidi Njokuani has dropped back-to-back bouts and was originally slated to face Geoff Neal before Álvarez stepped in on just 11 days' notice. He brings legitimate knockout power, with 15 of his 25 career wins coming by KO, and a notable 80-inch reach advantage at welterweight.

Joel Álvarez steps in on short notice coming off his first career submission loss to Yaroslav Amosov in May, which snapped a four-fight win streak. A converted lightweight now fighting at welterweight, 22 of his 23 career wins have come by finish, 5 by KO and 17 by submission.

Pick: Joel Álvarez

Method: Decision

Álvarez is nasty, and he's typically a fighter who only fights once a year when the stars align. The fact that he's out here on somewhat short notice is a good sign, it means he was already in camp and ready to go. He's a big, strong welterweight who hits hard, though probably not hard enough to knock these guys out at 170, but he has enough technical skill to outbox them on the feet. The concern is that he gasses. Expect Álvarez to win the first two rounds, fade, and hold on for a 29-28 decision.

Charles Johnson vs. Eduardo Henrique

Charles Johnson's bout was reshuffled just days out after his original opponent, Jose Ochoa, withdrew injured, hence the 130-lb catchweight. Johnson, 35, will be the UFC's most active fighter in 2026 with four appearances this year alone, averaging roughly 98 days between fights.

Eduardo Henrique is a former LFA champion and Contender Series winner stepping in on just four days' notice for his UFC debut, replacing the injured Ochoa. He also appeared on Season 33 of The Ultimate Fighter.

Pick: Charles Johnson

Method: KO/TKO

Johnson is a legendary gatekeeper of the flyweight division. He goes out there and knocks out Henrique early, a fighter who hasn't shown any significant skill or promise up to this point in his career. Four days' notice for a UFC debut against someone this active and experienced is too tall an order.

Donte Johnson vs. Eric McConico

Donte Johnson, "Lock Jaw," is an undersized 5'8" middleweight who spent his regional career at heavyweight and light heavyweight, making 185 lbs an ongoing experiment. He preserved his perfect 8-0 record with a split decision over Cody Brundage in March.

Eric McConico is a 36-year-old southpaw who has alternated wins and losses since his UFC debut, evening his promotional record to 2-2 with a clean decision over BJJ standout Rodolfo Vieira.

Pick: Donte Johnson

Method: KO/TKO

There was a lot of hype on Johnson coming off Contender Series, and some of it faded after the Brundage fight. He should still be the favorite here. Brundage is a better opponent than anyone McConico has faced, and Johnson is young in his career, likely improved after that last hiccup. Expect Johnson to make quick work of McConico.

Vicente Luque vs. Tresean Gore

Vicente Luque snapped a losing skid with a submission of Kelvin Gastelum in April and says he now feels like a middleweight "up-and-comer" after moving up from welterweight. He's lost five of his last eight overall but brings 11 knockouts and 10 submissions worth of finishing instinct.

Tresean Gore has struggled with consistency since joining the promotion in February 2022, but is coming off a resurgent submission win over Azamat Bekoev.

Pick: Tresean Gore

Method: Decision

Although he beat Gastelum in his last fight, Luque hasn't looked that good in his last several matchups. He's getting up there in age and doesn't have great standup anymore. Gore, meanwhile, is coming off a career-resurgent win over Bekoev. Luque's best chance is probably getting the fight to the ground and submitting Gore, but Gore isn't likely to let it go there, and instead pieces him up on the feet. Long night at the office for Luque.

Early Prelims Picks

Rafael Tobias vs. Lucas Fernando

Rafael Tobias earned his UFC deal via a first-round rear-naked choke over Jair de Oliveira on Dana White's Contender Series in 2025, but dropped his Octagon debut by unanimous decision to Diyar Nurgozhay. A tall frame with knockout power, he's viewed as a long-term project.

Lucas Fernando makes his first walk to the Octagon riding a four-fight win streak and a former LFA light heavyweight title reign built on elite, technical kickboxing.

Pick: Lucas Fernando

Method: KO/TKO

Not much to like about Tobias here, a loss to Diyar Nurgozhay is pretty pathetic in 2026. Fernando is making his debut too, but he's fought the likes of Bekoev and Cesar Almeida on the regional scene, a higher level of competition than anything Tobias has faced, and Tobias hasn't looked good since his UFC career started. Fernando goes out there and finishes Tobias within the first two rounds.

Neil Magny vs. Ramiz Brahimaj

Neil Magny, 39, looks to rebound from a first-round submission loss to Yaroslav Amosov, his third defeat in his last five fights. Still, he holds a striking-volume edge over Brahimaj at 3.40 vs. 2.29 significant strikes landed per minute.

Ramiz Brahimaj looks to bounce back after a three-fight win streak was snapped by a unanimous decision loss to Punahele Soriano in February. He's a submission threat, averaging 1.6 submission attempts per three rounds compared to Magny's 0.3.

Pick: Neil Magny

Method: KO/TKO

Magny is one of the greatest gatekeepers in the UFC welterweight division. Only fighters who are legitimately good manage to beat him. Brahimaj has mostly beaten suspect opposition in the UFC, while Magny recently beat Jake Matthews, even if the decision was controversial. Brahimaj just isn't good enough to get past this test. Expect Magny to dog this fight out and land an ugly finish in the second or third round.

Jeremiah Wells vs. Myktybek Orolbai

Jeremiah Wells, a Pennsylvania native who trains in Philadelphia at Renzo Gracie Philly, fights in front of a home crowd for UFC 330's opener, riding a bounce-back decision win over Themba Gorimbo.

Myktybek Orolbai enters on a scorching four-fight win streak, highlighted by a UFC welterweight-record 19 takedowns in a decision win over Chris Curtis. An accomplished wrestler with underrated power and a durable chin, he's quickly become one of the division's most dangerous rising talents, and the largest favorite on the entire card at -1050.

Pick: Myktybek Orolbai

Method: Submission

Hard to believe the curtain jerker for this event is Myktybek Orolbai while the main card has bullshit like Abdul-Malik on it, yet here we are. Orolbai smothers Wells with grappling early and gets a submission in either the first or second round.
