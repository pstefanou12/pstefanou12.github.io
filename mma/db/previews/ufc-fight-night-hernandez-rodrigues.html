Overview

UFC Fight Night: Hernandez vs. Rodrigues lands in Anthony Hernandez's backyard, and it's a card that leans heavily on his hometown story to carry it. The Sacramento native headlines against Gregory Rodrigues in a fight that's really about damage control: Hernandez is coming off the first stoppage loss of his career, a brutal TKO to Sean Strickland, and there are real questions here about whether "Fluffy" was ever as good as his unbeaten run suggested or whether he's just been fed soft matchmaking on the way up. Rodrigues, a legitimate power puncher who has ended three of his last four wins early, is the kind of live-power threat that tends to expose fighters whose chin has never really been tested. This has genuine finish-or-get-exposed energy on both sides.

The co-main is a sneaky-good heavyweight fight. Serghei Spivac looked like a shell of himself for a stretch, but he was frighteningly good in his last outing against Ante Delija, and there's a real case that Vitor Petrino, for all his athleticism, is still an oversized light heavyweight who hasn't been tested by real heavyweight grappling yet. That's a fun stylistic knot to untangle.

The rest of the main card is stacked with fighters trying to stop a slide. Reinier de Ridder and Roman Dolidze have each dropped back-to-back fights and meet in a light heavyweight bout that's as much about survival as anything else, with de Ridder a massive favorite. Mason Jones brings a seven-fight win streak into a clear step-up test against the unbeaten-in-the-UFC MarQuel Mederos, and Carli Judice looks like a legitimate prospect against an untested but undefeated Jeisla Chaves. The card closes its main portion with two UFC debuts colliding, as former NFL linebacker Anthony Wint takes on late-notice replacement Terrance Chatman in a fight the UFC clearly wants to use to keep Wint's Contender Series momentum rolling.

There's no title on the line here, but the main event carries real stakes for how the middleweight picture gets read going forward, and the undercard has more finishing power packed into it than most Fight Night cards get credit for.


Main Card Picks

Anthony Hernandez vs. Gregory Rodrigues

Anthony Hernandez (15-3) enters as a Sacramento native headlining in front of his home crowd, but he's coming off the first stoppage loss of his career, a TKO to now-champion Sean Strickland in February 2026. Before that he'd won eight straight, largely by suffocating grappling and relentless output, with wins over Roman Dolidze, Brendan Allen, and Michel Pereira.

Gregory Rodrigues (19-6) gets a second crack at a headline slot after coming up short in his first main-event opportunity against Jared Cannonier. He's won three straight since, two by knockout, most recently a first-round finish of Brunno Ferreira, and enters as a legitimate power threat to anyone in the middleweight top 15. Current odds favor Hernandez at -170, with the Sacramento crowd squarely behind him.

Stylistically this is Hernandez's volume and grappling against Rodrigues' one-shot power, and the version of Hernandez that shows up matters a lot: is he the fighter who steamrolled Allen and Pereira, or the one who got hurt and finished by Strickland and, earlier in his career, by Kevin Holland? Neither of those finishes came from fighters known as serious knockout artists, which raises real doubts about how much Hernandez can actually take.

Pick: Gregory Rodrigues

Method: KO/TKO

Hernandez has arguably been fast-tracked by matchmaking for most of his career, and the résumé has more soft spots than his unbeaten stretch suggested. He's been knocked out before, by Kevin Holland and now Strickland, and neither is a fighter renowned for one-punch power, which says more about Hernandez's own durability than it does about their power. He's shown he's soft to the body and historically struggles against opponents he can't drag into his own gameplan. Looking back, his best win by a wide margin is still Brendan Allen, and that's probably close to his real ceiling.

Rodrigues has real fight-ending power in either hand and doesn't need a long look to find it. This fight goes one of two ways: Rodrigues catches Hernandez clean in the first two rounds and ends it, or Hernandez gets his chain wrestling working against the fence and grinds out the later rounds as Rodrigues fades. The lean here is toward the former. Rodrigues finds the finish inside the first two rounds, and it's the kind of result that puts a real dent in Hernandez's hype.

Serghei Spivac vs. Vitor Petrino

Serghei Spivac (18-6) bounced back from a rough 1-2 stretch, capped by consecutive losses to Waldo Cortes-Acosta and Jailton Almeida, with a clear decision win over Ante Delija in February 2026, leaning on his suffocating top control and grappling pedigree. He's approached fight week with a notably quiet, business-like demeanor.

Vitor Petrino (14-2) has surged since moving up to heavyweight, running his streak to three straight, most recently a decision over Steven Asplund, and has said he sees a win over Spivac as the next step toward the heavyweight elite.

This is a clean stylistic clash. Petrino is the more dangerous, more athletic striker, while Spivac's wrestling and top control present a genuinely awkward puzzle for him. Petrino is a modest -150 favorite, and the market likes the fight to end early, with the Over on 1.5 rounds heavily favored.

Pick: Serghei Spivac

Method: Decision

Spivac hadn't inspired much confidence in recent memory, but he surprised in his last outing against Ante Delija, damn near finishing him inside the octagon. Petrino has had some soft matchups since arriving at heavyweight, and there's a real question about his gas tank: he looks like a guy who's carrying too much muscle for his own frame, size that may work against him over twenty-five minutes. Spivac, by contrast, has fought more heavyweights and has more experience against genuinely tough competition in the division.

Ultimately, Spivac wins this on the feet, not just on the mat, and roughs Petrino up at range over the course of the fight, riding his experience edge to a clear decision.

Reinier de Ridder vs. Roman Dolidze

Reinier de Ridder (21-4), a former two-division ONE Championship titleholder with 13 of his 21 wins by submission, has dropped back-to-back fights for the first time in his career, falling to Caio Borralho and Brendan Allen, and needs to arrest the skid.

Roman Dolidze (15-5) is also on a two-fight skid, most recently a decision loss to Christian Leroy Duncan, and owns eight of his 15 career wins by knockout, including a highlight-reel finish of Kevin Holland. Both men are moving up from middleweight into a division where a loss would mean a third straight setback.

Two names trending the wrong direction meeting to stop the bleeding. De Ridder is a massive -400 favorite over Dolidze at +316, with the market clearly expecting his size, grappling volume, and durability to carry the day at the new weight.

Pick: Reinier de Ridder

Method: Decision

Dolidze is a fairly one-dimensional fighter. He has real grappling and jiu-jitsu ability, but his striking isn't on the same level, and he doesn't blend the two disciplines together smoothly once the fight hits the ground. De Ridder, on the other hand, does that mixing much better: he's long, rangy, has some unorthodox striking tools, and combines it all effectively on the mat. There's also the sheer physical advantage of moving up a weight class to consider.

De Ridder gets the better of Dolidze across the three rounds and cruises to a 30-27 decision.

MarQuel Mederos vs. Mason Jones

MarQuel Mederos (11-1-1) remains unbeaten across four UFC fights, most recently battling Chris Padilla to a controversial majority draw at UFC 327 in April 2026. He's a +260 underdog against Jones.

Mason Jones (18-2) rides a red-hot seven-fight winning streak into Sacramento, most recently claiming Fight of the Night honors with a gritty unanimous-decision win over Axel Sola at UFC London. He's the -325 favorite.

Jones brings the longer, hotter streak and heavy betting favorite status; Mederos brings an unbeaten UFC run of his own, punctuated by that disputed draw against Padilla. On paper this is a clear form and momentum gap in Jones's favor.

Pick: Mason Jones

Method: KO/TKO

Jones is capable of putting things together at a high level in the octagon: he has the gas tank, the grappling, and the striking to threaten in every phase. Neither Chris Padilla nor Mederos have been especially impressive lately, and Mederos, notably, wasn't even able to beat Padilla. The expectation is that Jones beats Mederos up early, overwhelms him with pressure and volume in the first round, then gets him to the ground and finishes with ground and pound.

Carli Judice vs. Jeisla Chaves

Carli Judice (6-2) enters on a three-fight win streak that includes recent knockouts and a decision win over Juliana Miller in February 2026. She's a heavy -575 favorite.

Jeisla Chaves (8-0) is unbeaten and enters off a split-decision win over Yuneisy Duben in her UFC debut. She's a sizable +425 underdog trying to keep her perfect record intact against a much shorter price.

This is a clear betting favorite in Judice against an unbeaten but largely untested Chaves. Weight class is listed inconsistently across sources, flyweight in some, strawweight in others.

Pick: Carli Judice

Method: KO/TKO

Judice looks like a legitimate prospect at women's flyweight. There isn't much reason to be high on Chaves at this stage of her career, unbeaten record notwithstanding, and Judice should find the finish inside the first two rounds.

Anthony Wint vs. Terrance Chatman

Anthony Wint (7-0), a former NFL linebacker, makes his UFC debut off the back of a Dana White's Contender Series contract earned via a 35-second knockout of Matt Adams. He's finished his last three opponents in a combined 102 seconds.

Terrance Chatman (5-1) also makes his UFC debut, coming off a first-round leg-kick TKO of Juan Torres at Fury FC 121 in July 2026, a win that snapped his only career loss, a five-round split-decision title defeat to Myron Dennis in April 2026.

Both men are debuting, but the market sees this as lopsided: Wint is an overwhelming -1000 favorite, Chatman a +650 underdog, with the total set at Over/Under 1.5 rounds given Wint's finishing rate.

Pick: Anthony Wint

Method: KO/TKO

The UFC is clearly trying to build on the momentum Wint carried out of the Contender Series just weeks ago, and the matchmaking reflects it: Chatman is making his own promotional debut on short notice, which effectively makes this another Contender Series-style fight for Wint against someone who isn't fully prepared for the step up. Wint should end this inside the first round.

Prelims Picks

Jamall Emmers vs. Lerryan Douglas

At 37, Jamall Emmers (22-8) is on a two-fight win streak and holds a 5-4 UFC record. He steps in on short notice as a replacement opponent for Douglas.

Lerryan Douglas (14-5) is riding a six-fight winning streak with all six by knockout, including a sub-four-minute demolition of Julian Erosa in his Octagon debut. He wanted to return just three weeks after that win, but says two ranked featherweights turned the fight down over medical clearance issues before Emmers stepped in.

Douglas brings a perfect finishing streak and clear knockout power against an experienced but inconsistent veteran in Emmers, who was a late addition after Douglas's original opponents fell through.

Pick: Lerryan Douglas

Method: KO/TKO

This one's not entirely clear-cut, but Douglas is coming off a hot knockout streak, and Emmers is aging and has been streaky for a while now. Douglas goes out and puts Emmers away inside the first two rounds.

Kennedy Nzechukwu vs. Shamil Gaziev

Kennedy Nzechukwu (14-6-1) is trying to snap an inconsistent stretch that includes a controversial draw against Marcus "Buchecha" Almeida, a fight that featured a point deduction for an eye poke.

Shamil Gaziev (14-3) needs a bounce-back badly after back-to-back knockout losses, the most recent a Fight of the Night battle against Brando Peričić in May 2026.

Two heavyweights both trying to stop a slide, though Gaziev's is more acute given consecutive knockout losses. The market has Gaziev as a modest -135 favorite, with Nzechukwu at +110.

Pick: Shamil Gaziev

Method: KO/TKO

Gaziev looks like the kind of heavyweight who's going to struggle to break into the division's upper echelon, but he's a legitimate gatekeeper who turns away anyone without top-10 or top-5 aspirations. Nzechukwu, on the other hand, just isn't a good fighter. Gaziev has power and a chin; it isn't clear what Nzechukwu actually brings to counter that. Gaziev puts his lights out early.

Chris Padilla vs. Nasrat Haqparast

A Lynwood native, Chris Padilla (17-6-1) is unbeaten across five UFC fights (4-0-1), part of an eight-fight unbeaten run overall, and is coming off a controversial draw against MarQuel Mederos.

Nasrat Haqparast (18-6) is seeking redemption after a knockout loss last October and returns from a nine-month layoff. He owns the longer UFC résumé of the two.

This is a volume-vs-volume lightweight fight: Haqparast lands slightly more significant strikes per minute (6.36 to Padilla's 6.19) but with lower accuracy, while Padilla holds a real takedown-rate edge (1.13 per 15 minutes to Haqparast's 0.34) and a more varied finishing arsenal.

Pick: Chris Padilla

Method: KO/TKO

Haqparast's last knockout loss to Quillan Salkilld looked like a career-ender. It's genuinely surprising he's back fighting again; he was out cold on the ground for multiple minutes after the fight, and there's real reason to doubt his chin is the same. Padilla fights behind a high, tight guard that will make it hard for Haqparast to tag him clean. Expect Padilla to stay safe through the first round, then find Haqparast's chin and put him down in the second. If the chin really is gone, this turns into a beatdown.

Márcio Barbosa vs. Ryan Kuse

One of the most dangerous finishers on the card, Márcio Barbosa (18-2) has 15 wins by first-round finish, including a one-punch knockout of Dennis Buzukja in his UFC debut in April 2026. He earned his contract with a first-round TKO of Damon Wilson on Contender Series in August 2025.

Ryan Kuse (9-2) makes his UFC debut on a two-fight streak, most recently a highlight-reel head-kick knockout of Shad Walters at Karate Combat 62 in July 2026, following a second-round TKO of Leandro Camargo in April 2026.

Kuse is dangerous but stepping into the cage for the first time against a proven finisher with a deeper pedigree of high-level competition, one of the toughest tests in combat sports.

Pick: Márcio Barbosa

Method: KO/TKO

It's a little wild that the UFC keeps handing fighters debuts off the regional scene during Contender Series season, and this looks like another one of those spots. There isn't much known about Kuse, but Barbosa has already proven he's UFC-caliber. Barbosa goes out and puts him to sleep in the first or second round.

Gauge Young vs. Stanley Dorsainvil

Gauge Young (11-3) enters on a two-fight win streak, most recently a split-decision win over Thiago Moises in April 2026 following a unanimous decision over Maheshate in August 2025. He owns a 76% takedown-defense rate.

Stanley Dorsainvil (5-0) is undefeated and makes his UFC debut off pure momentum, most recently a third-round TKO over Dan Holt at LFA 233 in May 2026.

The more experienced Young is favored, but Dorsainvil's unbeaten, grappling-heavy résumé and his debut-fight adrenaline make this a live underdog spot, particularly given Young's need to lean on his takedown defense.

Pick: Gauge Young

Method: Decision

This is a tricky one to call, but a win over Thiago Moises is infinitely more impressive than anything Dorsainvil has done in his career to date. Young's experience should be the deciding factor here, and he out-crafts Dorsainvil over the three rounds.

Wesley Schultz vs. Jackson McVey

Wesley Schultz (9-3) snapped back-to-back setbacks, including a brutal first-round TKO loss to Damian Pinas in his UFC debut, with a gritty third-round submission win over Ben Johnston at UFC Perth in May 2026.

Jackson McVey (7-2) rebounded nicely in his last outing, submitting Sedriques Dumas via first-round D'Arce choke at UFC Fight Night in April 2026.

Both fighters enter off a single win following adversity. Prediction models can't separate them, and the odds reflect a genuinely close fight.

Pick: Jackson McVey

Method: KO/TKO

Schultz just doesn't look like a real threat here. McVey clips him once and Schultz goes down. First-round TKO for McVey.

Shanelle Dyer vs. Elise Reed

Shanelle Dyer (7-1) scored a dominant second-round TKO over Ravena Oliveira in her promotional debut in March 2026 and has declared her intention to become a world champion. She believes Elise Reed's striking style leaves her susceptible to a knockout. Reed (8-5) is looking to snap a two-fight skid after being finished via TKO by Denise Gomes in May 2025.

Dyer is a massive -850 favorite off an emphatic promotional debut win, against a veteran in Reed who has alternated wins and losses across her career and is fighting off a finish loss.

Pick: Shanelle Dyer

Method: KO/TKO

Dyer is a legitimate prospect with a real killer instinct. Reed is really nothing at this stage. Dyer knocks her out in the first round.
