Overview

UFC 329 is International Fight Week at T-Mobile Arena, and it is the most anticipated card in years. The reason is obvious: Conor McGregor is back. Whether you think that is compelling or concerning says everything about how you view this main event.

McGregor returns after nearly five years away from competition, the longest layoff of his career. He broke his leg at UFC 264 in July 2021, spent years mired in legal proceedings stemming from the Nikita Hand civil suit, and now shows up at 37 years old stepping into welterweight for the first time in his UFC career. His last four fights stand at 1-3. The line has him as roughly a -250 underdog against Max Holloway, which is remarkably generous given the circumstances. Holloway is a former UFC Featherweight Champion, the BMF title holder until losing it to Charles Oliveira in March, and one of the most prolific volume strikers the sport has ever produced. Both men are debuting at 170 pounds simultaneously. McGregor has a puncher's chance in the first two minutes. After that, expect a very long night for the Irishman.

The co-main event has genuine stakes attached to it. Benoît Saint-Denis is riding a four-fight finish streak and is the best he has ever been, coming off a brutal second-round TKO of Dan Hooker in Sydney. Paddy Pimblett took his first UFC loss against Justin Gaethje at UFC 324, but his submission game is deep enough to turn any grappling exchange into a serious problem for BSD. Saint-Denis's wrestling volume is elite, but his striking defense has been a consistent liability. Poirier threatened him with guillotines repeatedly, and Pimblett's jiu-jitsu is sharper than Poirier's. Every takedown attempt BSD throws carries an exit clause for Pimblett.

Gable Steveson makes his UFC debut at heavyweight against Elisha Ellison in what is best described as a promotional showcase. Steveson is an Olympic gold medalist and two-time NCAA Division I heavyweight champion with Jon Jones leading his training camp. He opens at -2800. Ellison is a 5-2 professional who lost his lone UFC appearance by KO. This is an introduction, not a competition.

Robert Whittaker makes his light heavyweight debut on the preliminary card against Nikita Krylov, a long-tenured UFC veteran with 16 submission wins and 13 knockouts. Whittaker abandoned the middleweight cut after it began visibly affecting his performances and says the move to 205 feels natural. Krylov is the kind of fighter who can end a scramble instantly, and scrambles are more common when you are adjusting to a new weight class. This is either a brilliant career reset or a cautionary tale about debut matchmaking.

Elsewhere on the card, Terrance McKinney brings his 100% finish rate into a guaranteed violence matchup with veteran King Green, and former bantamweight champion Cody Garbrandt finally collides with Adrian Yanez in a pure boxing fight fans have wanted for years. This card earns its International Fight Week billing.


Main Card Picks

Conor McGregor vs. Max Holloway

Conor McGregor (22-6) is making the most scrutinized return in MMA history. Five years out of competition, a broken leg, legal fallout from the Nikita Hand civil suit, and a first-ever move to welterweight at 37 years old. His last four fights went 1-3. He knocked out Poirier in January 2021, then got TKO'd by Poirier in July 2021 before the leg gave out entirely. His biggest asset has always been his left hand and his ability to land with precision early. The question nobody has a real answer to is how much of that remains after five years of inactivity and the kind of lifestyle that has surrounded his absence.

Max Holloway (27-9) is not in a slump. He is fresh off a BMF title loss to Charles Oliveira at UFC 326 in March, a decision that tells you very little about how he matches up with McGregor. Holloway averages 6.91 significant strikes per minute, won the BMF title with a highlight-reel KO of Justin Gaethje, and spent years as the featherweight champion dominating the best fighters alive. He is also debuting at welterweight here, but the extra weight is a bigger asset for a volume striker with a cardio engine than it is for a power puncher who has been inactive for half a decade. He originally beat McGregor by unanimous decision at featherweight back in August 2013.

Pick: Max Holloway

Method: KO/TKO

The -250 line on Max Holloway is a gift. The number implies a closer fight than the reality warrants. McGregor is returning from a nearly career-ending injury, a five-year layoff, and does so at 38 years old with a 1-3 record in his last four outings. His biggest wins came from early knockouts against opponents who were not volume strikers. Holloway is the antithesis of everything McGregor's power game is built to exploit: elite cardio, excellent footwork, and the ability to output relentlessly for as long as the fight lasts.

Analysts pointing to McGregor's speed and movement as a defense mechanism are missing the point. Those attributes are exactly what erodes first with age and inactivity, and McGregor has done nothing to suggest they are intact. Putting himself in vulnerable positions and trusting his reflexes to bail him out is not a real defensive strategy against a fighter throwing seven strikes per minute. If McGregor gets an early knockout, it will be the upset of the decade. If he does not, Holloway will take him apart round by round until the fight is stopped. McGregor does not want to compete at the highest level anymore; this feels like image rehabilitation more than a genuine championship push. It is going to be a bad night for the Irishman.

Benoît Saint Denis vs. Paddy Pimblett

Benoît Saint-Denis (17-3) is on the best run of his career, finishing four straight opponents by TKO. The most recent was a second-round stoppage of Dan Hooker in Sydney at UFC 325. After the fight he immediately called out the entire top five of the lightweight division. His game plan is built around wrestling volume and ground-and-pound, and he has the reach and physicality to impose that game plan on most opponents at 155. He enters as the betting favorite and with the momentum of a legitimate contender. The caveat is his striking defense: at UFC 299, Dustin Poirier exposed it badly and threatened him with guillotine chokes throughout, something a far more accomplished grappler like Pimblett will not overlook.

Paddy Pimblett (23-4) took the first UFC loss of his career against interim lightweight champion Justin Gaethje at UFC 324 in January, earning a Fight of the Night bonus in the process. He is a second-degree BJJ black belt with 10 submission wins, has beaten Michael Chandler by TKO, and has shown the ability to survive bad positions and find finishes from them. His 44% takedown defense rate is the number people flag against him, but the more important number is how many of those scrambles ended with him threatening an arm or a neck. He has publicly predicted he finishes Saint-Denis inside two rounds.

Pick: Paddy Pimblett

Method: KO/TKO

BSD has reinvented himself over the past two years and the four-fight finish streak is real, but the quality of opposition matters. He outgrappled Dan Hooker and finished some cans on the way up. His boxing is not his strong suit; Poirier embarrassed him on the feet at UFC 299 and nearly broke his neck with guillotine attempts throughout. Paddy's boxing is not world-class either, but it is better than BSD's, and his jiu-jitsu pedigree is in a different league than Poirier's.

Paddy Pimblett wins this fight by denying the takedowns and making BSD pay every time he tries to close the distance. Leg kicks, hooks on the outside, and if BSD shoots, a guillotine that is there every single time. Even against Beneil Dariush, one of the most durable chins at lightweight, BSD was cracked badly by a veteran striker. Paddy is going to piece him up on the feet, hurt him, and finish the fight with ground-and-pound after a knockdown. BSD's path involves getting top position and grinding, but Paddy is not going to give him clean shots to work from. This ends with Paddy's hand raised and another finish on the record.

Cory Sandhagen vs. Mario Bautista

Cory Sandhagen (18-6) challenged Merab Dvalishvili for the bantamweight title at UFC 320 in October 2025 and was dominated over five rounds, absorbing a record-setting 20 takedowns in a unanimous decision loss. He is 1-2 in his last three, ranked fifth at 135, and enters this rematch as the -150 favorite. He originally submitted Bautista with an armbar in the first round at UFC Brooklyn in 2019, and the seven years between that fight and this one have seen both men evolve substantially. Sandhagen is one of the most technically diverse strikers at bantamweight, with elite footwork and unconventional timing that makes him difficult to prepare for.

Mario Bautista (17-3) was on an eight-fight win streak before running into Umar Nurmagomedov at UFC 321, where he was outworked over three rounds in a unanimous decision. He rebounded with a second-round rear-naked choke of Vinicius Oliveira in February 2026 and enters this rematch as the +125 underdog. Ranked eighth at bantamweight, Bautista has sharpened his wrestling and submission game considerably since 2019, and he has made clear his game plan is built around pressure and a finish rather than points. His wrestling pressure is the variable that caused Sandhagen problems against Merab, and Bautista knows it.

Pick: Cory Sandhagen

Method: Decision

This is a close fight over three rounds, and Cory Sandhagen edges it. He is slightly better than Bautista in every area of the octagon. His footwork gives him angles that Bautista's forward pressure style has trouble cutting off, his striking is more technically refined, and his defensive grappling is a step above Bautista's offensive wrestling.

The Merab loss is a real data point but not a useful one for this matchup. Merab's wrestling volume is unprecedented in the division; Bautista is a good wrestler but not in that conversation. Bautista needs to impose physicality, keep Sandhagen from moving, and turn this into a pressure fight. Sandhagen's job is simpler: stay on the outside, use his footwork, and land the cleaner shots. He does not need to look spectacular here. He needs to be disciplined, and over three rounds that discipline is enough to get a 29-28 or 30-27 on the cards.

Brandon Royval vs. Lone'er Kavanagh

Brandon Royval (17-9) has lost two straight and the slide is real. He was knocked out in the first round by Manel Kape in December 2025, and before that lost a unanimous decision to Joshua Van at UFC 317 in what MMA Junkie named their 2025 Fight of the Year. Royval is a dangerous submission fighter with veteran experience and a 3-for-3 takedown accuracy rate when he commits, but he has been on the wrong end of every meaningful fight for over a year. A third straight loss ends his top-ten conversation entirely.

Lone'er Kavanagh (10-1) is the most compelling rising prospect at flyweight. He stepped up on short notice to beat former two-time flyweight champion Brandon Moreno by unanimous decision over five rounds at the February 28 Fight Night card in Mexico City, earning a Performance of the Night bonus. He is 10-1 overall with only one professional loss, is on a three-fight win streak, and has beaten Asu Almabayev and Charles Johnson on the way up. He stands 5'6" with a 67" reach against Royval's 5'9" and 68", and at 63% win probability per prediction algorithms, the data and the eye test agree: the trajectory strongly favors the younger man.

Pick: Lone'er Kavanagh

Method: Decision

Royval's manager is doing him no favors with this matchup. Royval thrives in cardio wars and five-round main events at the Apex where his grinding pace can wear opponents down. Putting him in a three-round fight against one of the most explosive and well-rounded young flyweights in the world is not the path back to the top ten.

Lone'er Kavanagh is an excellent striker who will pick Royval apart on the feet for three rounds. The real question is whether Kavanagh can deny serious takedown attempts and stay safe on the ground, and the answer based on everything we have seen is yes. Royval does not have a dominant grappling presence; he has solid submission instincts and solid boxing, but nothing elite in either department. Kavanagh does not need to go looking for trouble on the ground. He tags Royval with combinations, circles away, and lets the scorecards do the work. Royval cannot impose his will here, and a decision loss is the most likely outcome.

King Green vs. Terrance McKinney

King Green (35-17-1) is 39 years old and somehow better than he has ever been. He is on a three-fight win streak, demolished Jeremy Stephens at UFC 328, and is competing in his 31st UFC fight. Green's entire value proposition is durability, composure, and the accumulated experience of surviving every kind of violence the lightweight division has to offer. He does not panic, does not get sloppy, and does not get finished by shots that would end other fighters' nights. Those qualities are exactly what makes him a dangerous matchup for anyone playing recklessly, and Terrance McKinney plays recklessly by design.

McKinney (18-8) has a 100% finish rate across 26 professional fights, with 9 knockouts and 9 submissions. He has never seen the judges' scorecards in his career. He TKO'd Kyle Nelson in 24 seconds at UFC Seattle and has only made it out of the first round four times total. This fight is universally labeled a Fight of the Night candidate, and the label fits. McKinney is not interested in feeling out the first round. He is interested in ending the fight before Green's experience becomes a factor.

Pick: Terrance McKinney

Method: KO/TKO

This fight is not leaving the first round. Both men are going out swinging and the only question is who connects first.

Terrance McKinney gets it done. Green is on a solid run but the opposition has not been inspiring, and McKinney operates at a pace and ferocity that veteran composure has difficulty adjusting to when it hits you cold in the opening minute. McKinney is fast, violent, and accurate early. Green's hands will be down at some point in the opening exchange, and McKinney is the kind of fighter who does not need a second invitation. The fight ends in the first round with McKinney landing a clean shot and following it to the finish.

Prelims Picks

Robert Whittaker vs. Nikita Krylov

Robert Whittaker (26-9) is making his light heavyweight debut after a long career at middleweight where the 185-pound cut had been visibly affecting his performances. He has been open about it: the cut started costing him in fights, and the move to 205 "feels much more natural." The record at middleweight speaks for itself: two-time UFC champion, wins over Paulo Costa, Marvin Vettori, and Darren Till, consistently in the title picture against the best competition in the world. He KO'd Ikram Aliskerov in June 2024 to show his finishing power is still very much there. He enters as the clear favorite on debut at a new weight class.

Nikita Krylov (31-11) is a 34-year-old Ukrainian who has been in the UFC light heavyweight division for over a decade. He has 16 submission wins and 13 knockouts across his career, and his finishing rate means he does not need many opportunities to end a fight. He is the most experienced fighter at 205 in this matchup and is the kind of opponent who can capitalize instantly on a mistake in a scramble. He enters as the underdog against a fighter making his debut at the weight class.

Pick: Robert Whittaker

Method: Decision

Krylov's biggest wins in the UFC have come against opponents who were past their peak or made significant errors. Whenever he has been put in front of genuinely elite competition, he has lost. Whittaker is not just elite competition; he is elite competition with world-class technical boxing who does not historically rely on his physical attributes to win fights.

The light heavyweight division lacks the depth and quality of the middleweight division, and Whittaker's entire game has been built against the best of the best at 185. Moving to 205 means he is now physically bigger and more comfortable, not smaller and weaker. His recent win over Paulo Costa, who himself won his light heavyweight debut in spectacular fashion, demonstrates the cross-weight quality jump. Whittaker outboxes Krylov for three rounds, avoids the scrambles that would give Krylov his best opening, and takes a comfortable 30-27 decision.

Gable Steveson vs. Elisha Ellison

Gable Steveson (3-0) is one of the most decorated amateur wrestlers to enter MMA. He is an Olympic gold medalist in freestyle wrestling from the 2020 Tokyo Games and a two-time NCAA Division I heavyweight champion, and he has been training under Jon Jones heading into this UFC debut. He is 3-0 professionally with all three wins by TKO and opens as a -2800 betting favorite. This is a promotional showcase built around introducing Steveson to a mainstream audience, and the expectation is that the introduction goes smoothly and quickly.

Elisha Ellison (5-2) lost his lone previous UFC appearance to Brando Pericic by knockout. Prediction markets give him a 7.2% win probability. His presence on this card is not a competitive booking decision; it is a background role in someone else's debut story.

Pick: Gable Steveson

Method: KO/TKO

Elisha Ellison is being fed to Gable Steveson like a tune-up bout is supposed to work. Ellison probably should not be in the UFC, and Steveson's Olympic-level wrestling will put him on the canvas immediately. Once the takedown lands, the ground-and-pound ends the night quickly. Short work for the gold medalist.

Cody Garbrandt vs. Adrian Yañez

Cody Garbrandt (15-7) is a former UFC Bantamweight Champion making a comeback at 33 years old. He beat Xiao Long by unanimous decision at UFC 326 in March 2026 after back-to-back losses to Deiveson Figueiredo and Raoni Barcelos, and even that win was controversial: Xiao was docked two points for groin shots, and without the deductions the fight was closer than the final scorecards suggested. Garbrandt's power is legitimate and historically spectacular; he knocked out Dominick Cruz to win the title. The problem is that power has not been translating consistently, and his recent form against wrestlers and grapplers has exposed real defensive gaps.

Adrian Yanez (17-6-1) has not won a fight since May 2024, going back-to-back decision losses to Rob Font and Jonathan Martinez before a majority draw with Ricky Simon in March 2026. That draw is more misleading than the record suggests: Yanez was beating Simon badly, nuked his face for three rounds, and was arguably robbed of a TKO when the referee let Simon survive a finishing sequence by the bell. Yanez still has it. His boxing is quick and crisp, and a pure striking matchup against Garbrandt is exactly where he is most dangerous. This fight has been demanded by fans for years and both men acknowledge it was inevitable.

Pick: Adrian Yanez

Method: KO/TKO

When is the last time Cody Garbrandt looked genuinely good in a fight? His win over Xiao Long required two point deductions to secure, and even then many observers had Xiao winning. His recent form against high-level competition has been poor.

Adrian Yanez still has the tools to finish this fight inside two rounds. He is quick, technically sound on the feet, and has the kind of output that exposes opponents who are not moving well. Garbrandt is not moving well right now. Yanez picks his spots, lands clean, and the fight ends badly for the former champion. The draw against Simon was a robbery; Yanez was the better fighter that night and will be the better fighter here too.

Luke Riley vs. Kai Kamaka III

Luke Riley is a British featherweight still establishing himself in the UFC. His first two appearances in the promotion have shown genuine power in his hands, but he has not yet looked like a world beater at this level. Prediction algorithms give him a 67% win probability in this fight, which suggests he may be underrated relative to the matchup on paper.

Kai Kamaka III (9-4-1) is a Hawaiian veteran who requested this specific matchup, which usually means he sees a favorable stylistic picture. He stands 5'7" with a 69" reach and has submission wins on his record. He is a journeyman featherweight who has been in and out of the top 30, but his experience and ring craft give him a pathway against a younger fighter who has not yet demonstrated he can handle veterans who slow the fight down and mix levels.

Pick: Kai Kamaka III

Method: Decision

Luke Riley has power but is still young in his career and has not demonstrated the ability to impose his game against fighters with real experience. Kai Kamaka III requested this fight because he sees exactly that opening. Kamaka's approach here is veteran craft: mix in the clinch, fire a 1-2 and circle away, deny Riley the space to load up, and grind out a 29-28 decision by controlling the pace and not letting Riley play his own game. Riley's power is a real threat in the early going, but Kamaka's experience at managing that kind of danger and finding a path to the scorecards is what gets it done.

Early Prelims Picks

Tracy Cortez vs. Wang Cong

Tracy Cortez (12-3) is coming off back-to-back losses for the first time in her career, dropping to Erin Blanchfield by rear-naked choke in November 2025 and to Rose Namajunas by unanimous decision earlier that year. A third consecutive loss puts her roster spot in genuine jeopardy. She averages 3.8 significant strikes per minute, her grappling is her most reliable weapon, and she needs to get the fight away from the feet to have a path in this matchup.

Wang Cong (9-1, UFC 3-0) is one of the most impressive rising fighters in the women's flyweight division. She KO'd Victoria Leonardo in 62 seconds in August 2024, went on the road to Australia to beat Bruna Brasil with 104 strikes landed, and most recently dismantled Eduarda Moura with 147 strikes at 75% accuracy in February 2026. She averages 6.2 significant strikes per minute against Cortez's 3.8, a volume differential that is difficult to overcome on the feet. Her KO power is real and her volume is relentless.

Pick: Wang Cong

Method: Decision

In a pure kickboxing matchup, Wang Cong knocks Tracy Cortez out. The only thing that keeps this from being a finish is Cortez's grappling threat keeping Wang honest enough to not open up completely. But Wang has improved her cage work since her lone loss to Gabriella Fernandes and has learned to use the fence to stay on her feet and smother takedown attempts.

Cortez's only realistic path is to get a takedown and drag the fight away from where Wang does damage. If she cannot do that consistently, she absorbs volume for three rounds. Wang applies pressure, lands combinations to the body and head, and wins a comfortable decision. It is a bad night for Cortez unless the grappling clicks early and stays reliable.

Damian Pinas vs. César Almeida

Damian Pinas is a recent Contender Series product making only his second UFC appearance. He is identified as the more technical striker in this matchup: capable of cutting angles, moving in and out cleanly, and attacking to different levels. He carries a five-inch reach advantage over Almeida, which is a significant asset in a pure striking fight. Both men are orthodox strikers who want to stand and trade, and no grappling is expected from either side.

César Almeida (3-2 in the UFC) is a former Glory kickboxer with a come-forward pressure style built around physicality and high output. He is reach-disadvantaged in this matchup despite both men standing at 6'1", and his path to victory runs through closing the distance, forcing the inside game, and neutralizing Pinas's technical movement with volume and aggression. He is making his sixth walk to the UFC cage and has the experience to set traps and make athletic opponents make mistakes.

Pick: César Almeida

Method: KO/TKO

Pinas's reach and technical movement are real advantages, but they do not override experience when the stakes are this high. César Almeida is an accomplished kickboxer with proven wins over high-level competition in Glory. There is a ceiling on how far athleticism and raw movement take you against someone who has spent years learning to put combinations together, set traps, and manufacture openings in striking exchanges.

Since neither man is grappling, this is a pure kickboxing fight, and on that canvas Almeida's pedigree carries. Pinas will be moving and cutting angles, but Almeida's forward pressure will eventually corner him or catch him transitioning. He sets up the combination, Pinas gets caught, and the fight ends in the second round.

Farid Basharat vs. John Garza

Farid Basharat (15-0) holds the longest active win streak in the bantamweight division and is 6-0 in the UFC, the most recent win a split decision over Jean Matsumoto in February 2026. He enters this fight on the final bout of his contract and has said publicly that it is a gamble, with free agency on the line depending on what the UFC offers afterward. His original opponent, Ethyn Ewing, withdrew with an injury less than two weeks before the event, and John Garza stepped in as the replacement.

Garza is a short-notice replacement stepping in against an undefeated fighter on the final fight of his deal. He enters at a structural disadvantage in terms of preparation time, and Basharat's combination of clean wrestling and sharp finishing instincts across 15 professional fights gives him the tools to exploit that gap quickly.

Pick: Farid Basharat

Method: Decision

Farid Basharat wins this regardless of who steps in. He is 15-0 with a six-fight UFC win streak against a short-notice replacement. His contract situation adds narrative weight to the performance, but it does not change the outcome. Basharat controls the fight, earns the decision, and heads into free agency with a perfect professional record intact.

Ryan Gandra vs. Zachary Reese

Ryan Gandra (9-1) enters as the -140 to -170 betting favorite in an early prelim slot on a stacked International Fight Week card. He is 9-1 professionally and has shown enough in his regional career to earn the favor of the oddsmakers here.

Zachary Reese (10-3-1) is the +120 to +142 underdog and has previously appeared on a UFC prelim card. He has the kind of record that suggests competitive regional experience, and a win on IFW would be a significant career moment. Information on both fighters is limited at this level, but the odds tell the story clearly enough.

Pick: Ryan Gandra

Method: KO/TKO

Limited information on this matchup, but Ryan Gandra is the pick. Reese has not shown the goods to beat a live favorite at this stage of his career. Gandra gets him out of there.

Alessandro Costa vs. Cody Durden

Alessandro Costa is a Brazilian flyweight making an appearance on the IFW early prelim card. He brings the typical Brazilian jiu-jitsu foundation common to fighters out of that region and enters looking to make a statement on the biggest domestic card of the year.

Cody Durden (18-10-1) is a veteran flyweight who has been in and out of the UFC across multiple stints. He is a well-rounded fighter with submission wins on his record and the experience of having been tested at the UFC level multiple times. A win here keeps him relevant in the flyweight conversation; a loss likely signals the end of his latest UFC tenure.

Pick: Cody Durden

Method: Decision

Cody Durden's UFC experience and well-rounded game give him the edge over Alessandro Costa on a card where experience counts. Durden has been here before, knows the pace and pressure of a UFC fight night, and his grappling gives him a path to controlling the scorecards over three rounds.
