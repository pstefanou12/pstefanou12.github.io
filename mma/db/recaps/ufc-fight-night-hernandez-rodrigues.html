Summary

UFC Fight Night: Hernandez vs. Rodrigues was a legitimately solid show from top to bottom, even if it likely flew under a lot of people's radar.


The pace of these Fight Night cards continues to be a real selling point over the pay-per-view events. The prelims especially move a lot faster, with fewer commercial breaks and only a three-hour block instead of four, and the main card benefits from the same lighter ad load. The fights themselves were entertaining and delivered across the board. The one real knock on this event is that it needed more promotion behind it; it's very easy to see how a card this solid could have gone completely under the radar.


The main event lived up to its billing. Gregory Rodrigues outlasted Anthony Hernandez over five rounds in a genuine slugfest, stuffing takedown after takedown, chewing up Hernandez's lead leg with low kicks, and out-boxing him at range to take home a decision. It looks like Hernandez's ceiling may be his win over Brendan Allen, and time will tell how the rest of his career shapes up from here, but he'll need to be careful with his next matchup given how banged up he looked. Rodrigues, on the other hand, looked genuinely good, stopping a legitimate grappling threat and beating him up on the feet in the process, and there's a real case that he has the power to beat both Caio Borralho and Nassourdine Imavov at 185. The question now is whether his boxing defense is good enough to keep him from getting clipped by either of them.


Elsewhere on the card, Shanelle Dyer put a beating on Elise Reed that arguably should have been stopped before the third round even started, Reinier de Ridder landed something like 100 unanswered strikes to blow out Roman Dolidze, Chris Padilla pulled off a nasty comeback finish over Nasrat Haqparast after eating a long stretch of passive offense, and Shamil Gaziev needed one punch to end Kennedy Nzechukwu's night. Jamall Emmers also fraud-checked a hyped-up Lerryan Douglas with a first-round finish of his own.


Final Score: 7.4/10

Main Card

Gregory Rodrigues vs. Anthony Hernandez

Result: Decision (unanimous) (48–47, 49–46, 48–47) at R5 5:00

Anthony Hernandez shot for a takedown early, but Gregory Rodrigues denied it and used the fence to stay upright, and the first round settled into a stalemate of failed takedown attempts and clinch exchanges up against the cage, with Rodrigues starting to land the cleaner shots by the end of it. Round two turned in Rodrigues' favor in a big way: he started chewing up Hernandez's lead leg with low kicks, dropping him to the canvas multiple times and teeing off on him against the fence in between, even advancing to mount at one point. Hernandez, to his credit, dug deep and closed the round with a desperate flurry of punches and a takedown of his own.


The fight turned into a genuine grinding battle from there. Hernandez kept shooting for takedowns in rounds three and four, repeatedly getting the fight to the cage, while Rodrigues kept finding ways to separate, land elbows and low kicks, and wear Hernandez's legs down further. By round four, Rodrigues was walking Hernandez down early and landing clean, including a stinging right hand and a left hook, before Hernandez answered with a takedown to close the round. Round five was more of the same grinding, cage-clinch battle, with Rodrigues dropping Hernandez once more with a low kick and spending the closing stretch on top. The fight went the distance, and Rodrigues took home the decision.


It looks like Hernandez's ceiling might just be his win over Brendan Allen; only time will tell how the rest of his career shapes up, but he's clearly banged up and will need to be careful about his next matchup. Rodrigues, meanwhile, looked genuinely good, stopping a legitimate grappling threat and beating him up on the feet in the process. Styles make matchups, but there's a real case that Rodrigues has the power to beat both Caio Borralho and Nassourdine Imavov. The real question is whether his boxing defense is good enough to keep him from getting clipped and knocked out by either of them.

Vitor Petrino vs. Serghei Spivac

Result: Decision (unanimous) (30–27, 30–27, 30–27) at R3 5:00

Vitor Petrino got past Serghei Spivac on the scorecards, taking a clean unanimous decision over three rounds.

Reinier de Ridder vs. Roman Dolidze

Result: TKO (punches) at R1 4:01

Reinier de Ridder got Roman Dolidze down early and never let him up, unloading what looked like a hundred unanswered significant strikes before the referee finally stepped in to save Dolidze from further punishment.

MarQuel Mederos vs. Mason Jones

Result: TKO (elbows and punches) at R2 2:07

MarQuel Mederos pulled off the upset over Mason Jones, finishing him with elbows and punches just over two minutes into the second round.

Carli Judice vs. Jeisla Chaves

Result: TKO (front kick to the body and punches) at R1 1:39

Both women looked a little frantic early, but Carli Judice settled in first, pressing forward while Jeisla Chaves was stuck on the back foot. Judice hurt her with a body shot, and Chaves went down. That was it.


Yeah, Carli Judice is good. She made quick work of her opponent and got the job done in emphatic fashion. She should be given someone outside the rankings, or at the bottom of them, next, because she looks like she could be good enough to crack the top 5 at strawweight. The real test will be whether she can handle an elite grappler too.

Anthony Wint vs. Terrance Chatman

Result: Technical Submission (arm-triangle choke) at R1 4:29

Not much happened early, with both men exchanging without doing much damage. Anthony Wint tried for a takedown, but Terrance Chatman defended it and the two ended up tied up in over-unders against the fence. Wint switched to a single leg and got the fight to the ground, where Chatman tried a kimura from the bottom that he couldn't finish. Wint settled into side control, landing small shots without doing much damage, before locking up an arm-triangle choke that put Chatman out.


Wint is still super green. He needs to be fed a ton of cans before he's anywhere near ready to step in with any of the ranked heavyweights in the UFC. The fight itself was pretty boring to start, and it's good for Wint to get the win, but there's clearly a lot of work left to do.

Prelims

Jamall Emmers vs. Lerryan Douglas

Result: TKO (punches) at R1 3:38

Both fighters worked calf kicks early, with Jamall Emmers mostly on the back foot while Lerryan Douglas pressed forward and tried to cut off the cage. Emmers surged forward with a 1-2 off Douglas' guard, and Douglas nearly caught him back with a right hand of his own. It didn't matter. Emmers hurt Douglas badly and finished the fight with a TKO before the first round was over.


Nasty win from Emmers there. Douglas was supposed to be some kind of killer coming in, and instead he got completely fraud-checked. Wild result.

Shamil Gaziev vs. Kennedy Nzechukwu

Result: KO (punch) at R1 1:20

Shamil Gaziev needed just one clean right hand on the chin to put Kennedy Nzechukwu out cold.


That was exactly what the doctor ordered, an absolute masterclass from Gaziev. Nzechukwu, on the other hand, just isn't good.

Chris Padilla vs. Nasrat Haqparast

Result: Technical Submission (arm-triangle choke) at R3 4:59

Through the first two rounds, Nasrat Haqparast walked forward trying to engage, but Chris Padilla was way too passive, fighting off the back foot and spamming oblique kicks instead of committing to offense. Padilla came out more aggressive to start round three, though it didn't look like enough at first; he was still backing away and countering, without Haqparast ever landing anything of note. Then Padilla caught him with a nasty counter that put Haqparast down, and Padilla went in for the kill with ground and pound. Haqparast scrambled back to his feet, but Padilla smelled blood and kept walking him down, landing heavy strikes before locking up an arm-triangle choke on the feet and dragging Haqparast down to the canvas. Padilla never let go of the choke, and Haqparast was out cold at the bell.


Haqparast is done. He basically didn't take any real damage for the entire fight, but the second he got countered badly, he had nothing left to survive on. Quillan Salkilld did some serious damage to him last year, and it's time for him to hang it up. Padilla is a good fighter, he just takes way too long to get going out there. Still, that was a sick way to finish a fight.

Márcio Barbosa vs. Ryan Kuse

Result: TKO (punch) at R1 2:47

Ryan Kuse had absolutely no business being in there with Márcio Barbosa. He went for a takedown early, and the second Barbosa caught him with a clean shot, he was completely done.


Nasty knockout from Barbosa, but he was fighting a can out there. Kuse shouldn't be re-signed to the UFC after that; go back to the Contender Series if anything, because there's no way he gets another shot at this level.

Stanley Dorsainvil vs. Gauge Young

Result: Decision (unanimous) (29–28, 29–28, 29–28) at R3 5:00

Stanley Dorsainvil was the more aggressive fighter to start, moving forward while Gauge Young stayed light on his feet and worked off the back foot, but round one belonged to Young. He tagged Dorsainvil with an uppercut and a right hook, threw him to the canvas, and stayed on top landing ground and pound through a couple of scrambles before the two ended up tied against the fence. Dorsainvil got sloppy pushing forward and Young kept eating him up, though the round did see a moment of confusion when Dorsainvil claimed an eye poke that the referee waved off as nothing. Young closed the round by clipping Dorsainvil with a left hand that briefly put him down.


The fight tightened up considerably in round two, with both men trading and tagging each other much more evenly. Dorsainvil got to Young's back against the cage and landed some knees before Young reversed the position, and Dorsainvil closed the round with a nice right hand and a takedown. Round three saw Young get an early takedown of his own, but Dorsainvil got back up and pushed the fight to the cage, swinging for the fences from there. The two traded takedowns and reversals against the fence for a stretch, but by the closing minutes Dorsainvil was landing far more frequently and far more violently, and he did enough over the final two rounds to get the nod on the scorecards.


This fight is a good example of how flawed the scoring system in MMA can be. Officials need to be more liberal with handing out 10-8 rounds, because Young's first round was far more dominant than anything Dorsainvil did across the other two, and it isn't fair that those rounds get weighted equally against that kind of performance. In a situation like that, Dorsainvil arguably should have had to finish the fight rather than get the luxury of squeaking out a close 29-28 decision. Still, credit to him: he picked it up over the final two rounds and dogged out the win.

Jackson McVey vs. Wesley Schultz

Result: TKO (knee to the body and punches) at R1 4:13

Wesley Schultz charged forward early, and after a clinch exchange he even locked up a guillotine on Jackson McVey, who worked his way back to his feet. Schultz kept pressing McVey against the fence, and the two traded knees to the body before separating. From there McVey settled in, walking forward and tagging Schultz at will while Schultz did little besides retreat toward the cage. McVey closed the show with a step-in knee that put Schultz down for good.


Schultz just isn't a real fighter. It's good for McVey to get the win in a rematch of a fight from their amateur days, but this result doesn't really mean anything.

Shanelle Dyer vs. Elise Reed

Result: TKO (punches) at R3 1:42

Shanelle Dyer pressured Elise Reed heavily from the opening bell, landing a series of head kicks and a body kick before settling into a lighter calf-kick rhythm and closing the round with a spinning elbow attack against the fence. Round two was more of the same, Dyer walking Reed down and chopping at her legs, then piecing her up every time Reed tried to close the distance. She got Reed locked up against the fence and unloaded nasty knees in the clinch, leaving the skin above Reed's right eye completely bloodied by the end of the round. Round three was a formality. Dyer got Reed back against the fence and just poured it on until the referee finally stepped in to stop it.


Dyer should have finished this fight earlier; Reed had absolutely nothing for her the entire way. Reed drops to 8-6 with the loss, and reportedly her own coach asked her before the third round if she had anything left, and she said no. The fight should have ended right there. Dyer deserves someone outside the rankings, or at the very bottom of them, next; she looks like she could be good enough to crack the top 5 at strawweight. The one thing still left to find out is whether she can handle an elite grappler.
